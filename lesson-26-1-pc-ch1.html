🌱 Morphology: Prefixes pre-, re-, trans-, inter-
Prefixes that change the meaning of words
🌱
Morphology · Introduction
Prefixes pre-, re-, trans-, inter-
🌱
Teacher introduces prefixes pre-, re-, trans-, inter-
📋 Teacher Recording Notes
• Day 1 morphology = prefix review pre-, re-, trans-, inter- (DEFINE level)
• Teach meanings: pre- (before), re- (again/back), trans- (across), inter- (between)
• Example words: preview, return, transport, international
• Connect to Prince Caspian: the children were trans-ported across worlds
• ~2–3 minutes

Some prefixes change a word’s meaning by adding direction, time, or position. Today we learn four important ones:

📌 Key Prefixes

pre- = before  |  re- = again / back  |  trans- = across  |  inter- = between

Example words: preview (view before), return (turn back again), transport (carry across), international (between nations).

✏️ Grammar: Conjunctions (and / or)
Define the skill
✏️
Grammar · Conjunctions
Define the skill
✏️
Teacher introduces conjunctions and/or
📋 Teacher Recording Notes
• Day 1 grammar = conjunctions and/or (DEFINE level)
• Teach that conjunctions connect words, phrases, or sentences
• “And” adds ideas together; “or” gives choices
• Use examples from Ch. 1
• ~2–3 minutes

Conjunctions are words that connect. “And” adds ideas together. “Or” gives a choice between ideas.

“…the luggage, the seat, the platform, and the station had completely vanished.”
“Is it an island or the shore of the mainland?”

In the first sentence, “and” connects the last item in a list. In the second, “or” gives two possibilities.

🎮

Conjunction Spotter: Tap the conjunction in each sentence.

Were they on an island or a peninsula?

Spelling · Introduction
Fantasy Vocabulary Syllable Division
🔠
Teacher introduces syllable division for fantasy words
📋 Teacher Recording Notes
• Day 1 spelling = fantasy vocabulary syllable division (DEFINE level)
• Show words split into syllables. Discovery approach.
• ~2–3 minutes

Look at how these fantasy-related words break into syllables:

Group A — 2 Syllables

war·drobe

chan·nel

Group B — 3 Syllables

for·bid·den

ad·ven·ture

en·chant·ed

🔍 What do you notice?

Look at how these words break apart. Where do the syllable breaks happen? Do they break between consonants? Before consonants?

Most words break between two consonants that sit next to each other (VC/CV pattern): war-drobe, for-bid-den, ad-ven-ture.

When there’s only one consonant between two vowels, it usually goes with the next syllable (V/CV pattern): chan-nel.

Knowing where syllables break helps you spell and pronounce unfamiliar words — especially the big fantasy words you’ll meet in Prince Caspian.
