🌱 Morphology: Word-Building — Use
Choose the right prefix or suffix to build a real word.

This week we review roots from across all the novels you’ve read this year. Each root has shown up in words from your reading. Match each word to its root — and think about how the root helps you understand what the word means.

📌 Root Fill-in-the-Blank: Spiral Review

transport  |  export  |  distract  |  extract
inscription  |  visible  |  infinite  |  final

Which words contain the root port (carry)?
✅ transport = trans + port (carry across) — to carry goods or people from one place to another. export = ex + port (carry out) — to send goods out of a country.
Which words contain the root tract (pull / draw)?
✅ distract = dis + tract (pull away) — to pull attention away. extract = ex + tract (pull out) — to pull something out.
Which words contain the root fin (end / limit)?
✅ infinite = in + fin + ite (not limited) — having no end. final = fin + al (of the end) — last; coming at the end.

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“Annemarie relaxed the clenched fingers of her right hand, which still gripped Ellen’s necklace.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
This sentence comes from Number the Stars. Annemarie has just been through something frightening. What is happening in this moment? Why is she still gripping Ellen’s necklace? What does that detail tell you about how much Ellen matters to her?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice the contrast between “relaxed” and “clenched.” The word “clenched” tells us her hand was tight — tense, desperate, holding on. The word “relaxed” tells us the danger is over. But the necklace is still there. How does the contrast between “relaxed” and “clenched” show what Annemarie is feeling — both then and now?
🖊️ Mimic
Write your own sentence using the same pattern: a character releases tension, but something still shows what they were holding on to.
[Character] [released/loosened/relaxed] the [tense body part], which still [held/gripped/clutched] [object].
