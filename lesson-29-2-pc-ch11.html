✏️ Grammar: Commas in Dates & Places — Identify
Click where the comma belongs in each sentence.
📌 The Rule

Dates: Put a comma between the day and the year. If the sentence continues after the year, add another comma. On March 3, 1949, the children returned to Narnia.

Places: Put a comma between a city/place and a larger region or country. They sailed from Cair Paravel, Narnia.

Tap the spot in each sentence where a comma belongs. Some sentences need two commas.

On March 3 [comma] 1949 the children returned to Narnia.
Aslan was seen near Aslan’s How [comma] the Western Wild.
On June 15 [comma] 2026 we will finish reading Prince Caspian.
The old kings once ruled from Cair Paravel [comma] Narnia.
Trumpkin was born in Narnia [comma] the land beyond the wardrobe.

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“The trees were all around them; they were closing in, towering overhead.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
What is happening here? The trees of Narnia are waking up around the children. Why would Lewis describe this moment so simply — just two short clauses — instead of giving it a long, dramatic description?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice the semicolon. Lewis joins two independent clauses with a semicolon instead of a period. Both clauses describe the same scene from slightly different angles: one about the trees surrounding them, one about the trees above them. How does linking them this way change the feeling of the sentence?
🖊️ Mimic
Write your own sentence using a semicolon to join two related clauses. Describe a place, a moment, or a feeling using this pattern.
[First observation]; [second observation that adds to the first].
