📌 Cumulative Spelling Words — Week 30 Unit Review

interrupt  |  transform  |  conduct  |  sufficient  |  treachery
coronation  |  honourable  |  proclaimed  |  surrender  |  lineage

Which word contains the root rupt (break / burst)?
✅ interrupt = inter (between) + rupt (break) — to break into something in the middle.
Which word contains the root form (shape / change)?
✅ transform = trans (across) + form (shape) — to change shape or nature completely.
Which word contains the root duct (lead)?
✅ conduct = con (together) + duct (lead) — to lead or direct; also one’s behaviour.

📚 What Good Readers Do
Your reading lens for today

Good readers think about endings. The last chapter of a book is not just the last chapter — it is where the author resolves what matters and asks us to let go of what cannot stay.

As you read Chapter 15, notice what gets resolved and what the characters must give up. The biggest moments may not be the coronation — they may be the farewell.

🧠 As you read today, ask yourself:

Aslan tells Caspian that feeling “not sufficient” is actually proof he IS ready. What does Aslan mean? Why is doubt a sign of good leadership?

The mice all offer to cut off their tails if Reepicheep cannot have his. Aslan says, “You have conquered me.” What conquers Aslan — and what does this tell us about what he values most?

Peter and Susan learn they will never return to Narnia. How does the ending make you feel? What do you think C. S. Lewis wants you to carry away?

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“You come of the Lord Adam and the Lady Eve. And that is both honour enough to erect the head of the poorest beggar, and shame enough to bow the shoulders of the greatest emperor on earth.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
Aslan says this to Caspian as he is about to be crowned king. Caspian has just wished he came from a more honourable lineage. Aslan’s answer is breathtaking: every human being carries both great honour and great shame simply by being human — a son of Adam or daughter of Eve. What is Aslan telling Caspian? Why is this the right thing to say at this moment?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice the balanced parallel structure: “both honour enough to erect the head of the poorest beggar, and shame enough to bow the shoulders of the greatest emperor.” Lewis places two opposites side by side in identical grammatical patterns — honour / shame, erect / bow, poorest beggar / greatest emperor. Each pair is the mirror image of the other. Why is this balance so powerful? What would be lost if Lewis had written it as a plain statement instead?
🖊️ Mimic
Write your own sentence using the same “both ___ enough to ___, and ___ enough to ___” balanced structure. Think of two opposite qualities — strength and weakness, joy and sorrow, courage and fear — and set them side by side.
That is both ___ enough to ___, and ___ enough to ___.
