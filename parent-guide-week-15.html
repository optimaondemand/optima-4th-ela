This Week at a Glance

Before we start our next novel, Number the Stars by Lois Lowry, your student spends a week on the real history behind it. Instead of chapters, each lesson has a short nonfiction reading adapted from the U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum: how World War II began, what happened when Germany took over Denmark, the yellow star Jewish people were forced to wear, and how ordinary Danes saved more than 7,200 Jewish neighbors in October 1943.

💛 A heads-up for families
These readings mention the Nazi persecution of Jewish people, including arrests and concentration camps. Everything is written for fourth graders, and the focus is on courage and rescue. Your student may have questions. It’s fine to say “that’s a hard part of history” and talk it through together.
Reading skill
Building background knowledge. Knowing the real history makes historical fiction easier to understand and more powerful to read.
Big idea
The courage of ordinary people. Fishermen, doctors, police, and neighbors each did one brave thing, and together they saved thousands.

Words to Know

Use them at home!
resistancepushing back against something; in WWII, secret groups who fought the Nazis
occupationwhen one country takes over and controls another
deportto force someone to leave a country, usually against their will
rescueto save someone from danger
Word parts
trans- means “across.” pro- means “forward.”transport · translate · progress · protect
Spelling
Vowel teams in history words: two vowels working together to make one sound.achieve · campaign · neutral
Grammar
Subject and object pronouns. Subjects do the action (I, she, they); objects receive it (me, her, them).She hid them. They helped us.

By Heart: A New Poem

There will come soft rains and the smell of the ground,
And swallows circling with their shimmering sound;
And frogs in the pools singing at night,
And wild plum-trees in tremulous white;Sara Teasdale, “There Will Come Soft Rains” (1920), first stanza

This week starts a new poem to memorize, which carries through the Number the Stars unit. It comes up on Days 2 and 4, and each time a few more words fade from the screen.

How to help: Read it aloud together this week. Teasdale wrote it during World War I. It imagines nature carrying on peacefully no matter what people do.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did World War II begin, and who fought against Germany?Listen for: Germany attacked Poland in 1939; the Allies (Britain, France, later the U.S. and Soviet Union) fought back.
  2. How was Denmark different from other countries the Nazis took over?Listen for: the king stayed, the government kept running, and Denmark refused to make Jewish people wear the star.
  3. Who helped in the October 1943 rescue, and how?Listen for: doctors hid people as “patients,” fishermen gave their boats, police looked the other way.
  4. What do you think it took for ordinary people to be that brave?Listen for: any honest thinking. There’s no single right answer.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Find it on a map. Locate Denmark and Sweden. How narrow is the water the fishing boats crossed?
  • Pronoun swap. “Anna and me went” or “Anna and I went”? Take out “Anna” and listen.
  • Everyday courage. Talk about a time someone in your family stood up for a friend or neighbor.
