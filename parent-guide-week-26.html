This Week at a Glance

A new book begins. Peter, Susan, Edmund and Lucy are waiting at a train station when they are pulled away to a wild island. They find an old orchard and a ruined castle, and work out that it is their own Cair Paravel from their first adventure in Narnia: hundreds of Narnian years have passed while only one passed in England. Then they rescue a Dwarf from two soldiers, and he tells them he is a messenger of “King Caspian.” Each day your student also writes a short Writing Connection.

💛 A heads-up for families
In Chapter 3, two soldiers are about to drown a tied-up Dwarf. Susan shoots an arrow that knocks one soldier into the water; both soldiers get away, and nobody is killed. The full list for the book is on the unit guide.
Reading skill
Reading a fantasy setting. Your student collects place clues (what the children see and touch) and time clues (what has crumbled or grown wild), then says where and when the children are in one sentence.
Big idea
Coming back to a place that has changed. The children remember a castle full of life. They find a ruin, and have to figure out what happened while they were gone.

“This must have been an orchard — long, long ago…”Susan, Chapter 1

Words to Know

Use them at home!
junctiona place where things meet, especially where train lines split
thicketa dense group of bushes or small trees
bramblesthorny bushes with prickly stems
inhabitedlived in
Word parts
Prefixes that tell when and where: pre- (before), re- (again, back), trans- (across), inter- (between).preview · return · transport · international
Spelling
Long words, part by part. Peel off the prefix and suffix first; what’s left is a word you know, and the syllables split easily.un · familiar · transport · ed
Grammar
And and or. And joins ideas that are both true; or offers a choice. Too many ands in a row make every idea sound the same.stay in the castle or ride into the woods

On Days 1 and 3, each word your student builds sets a treasure back in the castle’s old treasure chamber, four a day, until every torch is lit.
🏛️ Seven Pillars Watch, after reading
Real moments from the chapter, and your student taps which of the seven OAO pillars each one shows: honesty, respect, courtesy, responsibility, courage, service or perseverance. On Day 4, they name the week’s strongest pillar and prove it.

Before reading, your student picks one of three lenses and reads through it. One line at the end of the questions asks what it helped them notice.
🪶 A new poem to learn
“The Forest Remembers,” four lines about a land that remembers the people who loved it.
✎ Copia: Say It Three Ways
On Day 1, your student rewrites “The children found an old stone wall” three ways: a new opening, a stronger verb, and an added detail.

Writing This Week

Writing Connection

No long essay in this unit. Each day ends with a short Writing Connection: Day 1 compares the train station with the island, Day 2 describes a place that changed, Day 3 retells a moment from Trumpkin’s point of view, and Day 4 names one thing C. S. Lewis does well as a writer, with an example.

By Heart: the Unit Poem

The stones still stand where towers fell,
The river hums a half-lost spell,
The trees lean close as if to say,
“We knew you’d find your way back someday.”“The Forest Remembers,” written for our Prince Caspian lessons

A new poem for a new book. On Day 2 your student reads it and starts learning it; on Day 4 a few words fade from the screen.

How to help: Ask your student who “you” is in the last line. Then ask how the poem fits the children coming back to Cair Paravel.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did the children get to Narnia this time?Listen for: they were sitting at a train station and felt a pull; then they were standing in a thick wood.
  2. How did they figure out the ruin was Cair Paravel?Listen for: Peter’s clues, such as the shape of the hall, the well, the chess-knight, and the orchard.
  3. Why did the castle look so old if they had only been gone a year?Listen for: Edmund’s idea that time in Narnia runs differently, so hundreds of years passed there.
  4. What gifts did they find in the treasure chamber? What was missing?Listen for: Lucy’s cordial, Susan’s bow, Peter’s sword and shield; Susan’s horn was gone.
  5. Who did the children rescue, and what did he tell them?Listen for: a Dwarf the soldiers were about to drown; he is a messenger of King Caspian.
  6. Which pillar did you see most this week?Listen for: a pillar and a real moment from Chapters 1–3.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Visit a place from long ago (an old house, a park, a photo of where you grew up) and talk about what time changed.
  • Ask your student to predict who King Caspian is, and write it down to check later.
  • Look at pictures of real castle ruins and find the hall, the tower and the well.
