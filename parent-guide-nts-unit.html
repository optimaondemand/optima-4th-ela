About the Book

Number the Stars (1989) won the Newbery Medal. It is set in Copenhagen, Denmark, in 1943, during the German occupation of World War II. Ten-year-old Annemarie Johansen’s best friend, Ellen Rosen, is Jewish. When the Nazis begin rounding up Denmark’s Jewish families, the Johansens hide Ellen and help her family escape by fishing boat to Sweden, and Annemarie finds out how brave she can be.

Annemarie and Ellen are made up, but the rescue is real. In October 1943, ordinary Danes helped about 7,200 Jewish neighbors reach safety in Sweden. We read it because it is a gripping, short novel that shows real history through a child’s eyes, and because it gives fourth graders a lot to think and talk about: courage, loyalty, friendship, and doing the right thing when it’s dangerous.

“It is much easier to be brave if you do not know everything.”Uncle Henrik, Chapter 9

How a Week Works

Every week has four lessons, one fluency reading, and a Check-In. Each lesson follows the same path, so your student always knows what comes next. Across the four days, the skills build:

Day 1Learn it: define and explain
Day 2Find it: identify and sort
Day 3Use it: write and apply
Day 4Prove it: revise and explain
  • 📚What Good Readers Do: one reading habit to watch for in the chapter
  • ✏️Daily Oral Language: fix a sentence by copying a correct model
  • 🔤Word study: Latin roots, spelling patterns, and vocabulary from the chapters
  • 🧩Grammar: a pronoun-and-apostrophe unit, taught with sentences from the book
  • 🛡️Courage Watch: each chapter names one kind of courage to look for
  • 🗺️Annemarie’s Path: a map that follows the story through Denmark
  • 🎧Grete, the narrator: a Danish storyteller who introduces lessons in short audio clips
  • 🏛️Real history: museum objects, then-and-now photos, and real rescuers
  • 🎙️Fluency: a short passage read three times, then recorded
  • ✍️Writer’s Workshop: one opinion essay built across the unit

Your Student’s Opinion Essay

Writer’s Workshop

Across the unit, your student writes one opinion essay about courage in Number the Stars: a claim, two or three reasons, a quote from the book for each, and a conclusion. Each week adds one piece, with an example writer named Sam showing how it’s done.

Wk 15Fact vs. opinion
Wk 16Claim + reasons
Wk 17Evidence + drafting
Wk 18Elaborate
Wk 19Publish

Drafting starts in Week 17. The essay box saves in the browser on the computer your student uses, so if your student switches computers, help them keep a copy somewhere else too (a document or a printout).

By Heart: The Unit Poem

Robins will wear their feathery fire
Whistling their whims on a low fence-wire;
And not one will know of the war, not one
Will care at last when it is done.Sara Teasdale, “There Will Come Soft Rains” (1920), lines 5–8

Teasdale wrote this poem during World War I. It imagines nature carrying on peacefully, whatever people do. Your student reads the whole poem in Week 15 and memorizes these four lines. Each time the poem comes up, a few more words fade from the screen.

How to help: Ask to hear it now and then. By Week 19 they should be able to say all four lines to you.

How Learning Is Checked

Every lesson
Chapter questions answered with evidence from the book, plus word-study and writing responses, submitted in Canvas.
Every week
A fluency recording, plus a Check-In (about 20–30 minutes) covering grammar, word parts, spelling, reading, and one short writing task.
End of unit
The published opinion essay about courage, compared with what Lowry says in her Afterword. Your student also reads it aloud as the Week 19 fluency recording.

Lessons have built-in self-checks and hint buttons, so students can see right away whether they’re on track.

Good to Know Ahead of Time

Weeks 15–19 · The history
The unit deals with the Nazi persecution of Jewish people, including arrests and concentration camps. Everything is written for fourth graders, and the focus is on courage and rescue. It’s fine to say “that’s a hard part of history” and talk it through.
Week 19 · Chapter 17
The last chapter reveals that Annemarie’s sister, Lise, was in the Resistance and was killed by the Nazis, and that Peter was later executed. It may help to read it together or check in afterward.
Week 18 · Chapter 10
Soldiers burst into a nighttime gathering and threaten the family. It’s the tensest scene in the book. Peter also reads Psalm 147 aloud, which gives the novel its title.
Week 19 · The Afterword
Lowry ends with part of a real letter written by a young Danish Resistance fighter before his death. She uses it to show what the people in the book were fighting for.

How to Help (Without Doing It for Them)

Try this

  • Ask “Where in the book does it say that?” when your student answers a question.
  • Point them to the hint buttons and self-checks before you step in.
  • Ask the questions in each weekly guide. Talking it through first makes the writing easier.
  • Be the audience for the fluency reading, the poem, and the finished essay.

Try to avoid

  • Typing or wording answers for them. Teachers need to see your student’s own thinking.
  • Fixing their essay draft. Revising and editing are lessons of their own in Week 19.
  • Telling them how the book ends. The suspense is part of the point.
