This Week at a Glance

No new reading this week. Your student puts the year’s five books side by side: Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH, Anne of Green Gables, Number the Stars, Freedom Train and Prince Caspian. Day 1 they choose a theme, Day 2 they find it in two characters from two different books, Day 3 they write a comparison paragraph with evidence from both, and Day 4 they say what the books together taught them about people. Opti, the owl from the start of the year, is the guide again.

💛 A tip for families
Keep the books (or the reading journal) within reach this week. The best evidence is a real moment your student can point to, not a memory of one.
Reading skill
Comparing themes across texts. Your student shows how two characters in different stories share a big idea, and how they show it in different ways.
Big idea
Stories teach us about people. Courage, loyalty and belonging look different in a rat, a red-haired orphan, a Danish girl, Harriet Tubman and a young king.

“That’s all that brave means—not thinking about the dangers. Just thinking about what you must do.”Uncle Henrik, Number the Stars

Word parts
A year of word parts. Find a part you know in a long word and the word starts to explain itself.port (carry) · spect (look) · rupt (break) · pre (before)
Spelling
Check the pattern. Before handing in writing, check the rules one at a time: drop the silent e, change y to i, double the consonant.hope → hoping · happy → happier · stop → stopped
Grammar
Every mark has a job. Capitals start and name; commas join or separate; quotation marks hold someone’s exact words.Although Harriet and Anne faced different struggles, they both refused to give up.

Writing This Week

Comparison paragraph

One paragraph, built across the week. It opens with a theme sentence, gives a real moment from each of two books as evidence, explains how the characters are alike and how they are different, and ends by saying what the theme shows about people. Next week your student polishes it for the year-end portfolio.

🪶

Poem of the Week

There is no Frigate like a Book
To take us Lands away,
Nor any Coursers like a Page
Of prancing Poetry –Emily Dickinson, first stanza

A frigate is a sailing ship and a courser is a fast horse. Dickinson is saying a book carries you farther than either.

How to help: Ask which of the five books took your student the farthest away, and why.

💬

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What theme did you choose?Listen for: a big idea about life, like courage, loyalty or belonging, not just a topic.
  2. Which two characters show it?Listen for: two characters from two different books.
  3. What is one moment that proves it for each?Listen for: a specific thing the character did or said.
  4. How are the two characters different, even though they share the theme?Listen for: different times, places, dangers or reasons for acting.
  5. What do the books together say about people?Listen for: one clear sentence your student believes.
  6. Which pillar did the characters show most this year?Listen for: a pillar and a real moment from any of the five books.
