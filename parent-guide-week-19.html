This Week at a Glance

Mama comes home with a broken ankle, and a packet meant for Uncle Henrik’s boat has been left behind. Annemarie has to run it through the dark woods alone. When soldiers with dogs stop her, she plays a silly little girl and gets past them. The story then jumps ahead to the end of the war, and Lois Lowry’s Afterword tells readers which parts were real. Your student finishes and publishes their opinion essay.

💛 A heads-up for families
Chapter 17 reveals that Lise was part of the Danish Resistance and was killed by the Nazis, and that Peter was later executed. The Afterword ends with part of a real letter a young Danish Resistance fighter wrote before his death. It’s handled gently, but it is the saddest part of the book. Reading Chapter 17 together, or checking in afterward, can help.
Reading skill
Comparing historical fiction with history. Your student sorts what Lowry invented from what really happened, and thinks about what a novel can do that a history book can’t.
Big idea
Ordinary people, extraordinary choices. A fisherman, a doctor, a ten-year-old girl: the rescue worked because a whole country chose to help.

“I will tell you just a little. So that you will be safer.”Uncle Henrik, Chapter 16

Words to Know

Use them at home!
conformto match a shape or rule; to fit in
reflectto think back; or, to bounce back light
compareto notice what is the same and different
concludeto finish; to come to a final idea
Word parts
form means “shape.” flect and flex mean “to bend.”conform · reform · reflect · flexible
Spelling
Homophones in context. They sound the same, so only the meaning of the sentence tells you which spelling to use.their · there · they’re · new · knew
Grammar
Homophones in writing. Your student practices choosing the right one when editing.The soldiers passed (not past) the door.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did Annemarie get past the soldiers?Listen for: she acted like a chatty, silly little girl, so they didn’t take her seriously.
  2. What was in the handkerchief, and why did it matter?Listen for: something that ruined the dogs’ sense of smell, so they couldn’t find the people hidden on the boat.
  3. Which parts of the book really happened?Listen for: the rescue by fishing boat, the handkerchiefs, the king riding through Copenhagen; Annemarie and Ellen are made up but inspired by a real friend’s childhood.
  4. Read me your essay. What’s your strongest reason?Listen for: be the audience. A real listener makes the fluency recording better.

What Gets Turned In

  • Each lesson: 3 chapter questions plus word-study and writing responses, submitted in Canvas
  • Final essay: published by Day 4
  • Fluency: a recording of your student reading their own essay
  • Check-In: at the end of the week

Done well looks like: a finished essay with a clear claim, two reasons, a quote for each, and a conclusion, read aloud with expression.
