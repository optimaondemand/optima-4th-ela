🧭 How the Lesson Pages Work

  • 🗂️Four tabs, in order: Warm-Up → Word Study → Reading → Assignment. A button at the bottom of each tab moves to the next.
  • 🔊Two buttons at the top: Read aloud opens the page reader (with a speed control), and Lesson details shows the unit, week, chapter and standards.
  • 📝Every answer goes on the page. There’s a box for each one, including the Daily Oral Language sentence, and a word count under it shows how much is expected.
  • ▶️Start here: a gold drawer at the very top of every page holds a one-minute video on how to use the lesson.
  • 👆One step at a time: each activity opens with a short “What to do” list, and the page shows one step at a time. Tap 👆 Next to move on, or ◂ Back to look again.

  • 📓Download your ELA journal for the day at the bottom of the Assignment tab saves everything as one page. Use it (or Copy) to submit in Canvas. A lesson isn’t turned in until it’s submitted there.
  • 🎙️Fluency is a separate page in the Canvas module, because recording doesn’t work inside a lesson. Keep the book nearby.

This Week at a Glance

Lucy wakes in the night and finds Aslan. He tells her to wake the others and follow him, even though they won’t be able to see him. She does. One by one, as they follow, the others begin to see him too, and Susan and Peter say they are sorry. Aslan roars, and the trees of Narnia wake. Meanwhile, in Aslan’s How, Nikabrik brings a Hag and a Werewolf to Caspian’s council and proposes calling back the White Witch. Peter, Edmund and Trumpkin arrive in time. Each day your student also writes a short Writing Connection.

💛 A heads-up for families
Chapter 12 is the darkest so far. In a fight in the dark, Nikabrik, the Hag and the Werewolf are killed, and Caspian is bitten. Lewis keeps it brief, and Caspian speaks kindly of Nikabrik afterward.
Reading skill
Theme through repeated choices. Your student notices choices that echo each other (whom to trust, whether to admit you were wrong) and builds a theme sentence from them.
Big idea
Trust, and what it costs. Lucy trusts what only she can see. Others admit they were wrong. Nikabrik trusts the wrong power.

“Every year you grow, you will find me bigger.”Aslan, Chapter 10

Word parts
Latin bases: rupt (break), duct / duce (lead), form (shape).interrupt · aqueduct · formation
Spelling
Word parts, review. Find the Greek or Latin part you know and spell it the same way every time.port·able · spect·a·tor
Grammar
Commas in dates and places. A comma between the day and the year, between a town and its bigger place, and one more after them if the sentence goes on. (Narnia has no calendar, so practice dates are made up.)June 12, 1941 · Dover, Kent

Writing This Week

Writing Connection

This week builds toward a theme paragraph. Day 1 is three or four sentences about Aslan’s words, “Nobody is ever told that”; Days 2 and 3 practice Lewis’s sentence patterns (the far-then-near sentence about the trees, and Trufflehunter’s “I’m a … and we …”); Day 4 is a draft theme paragraph with two examples from Chapters 10–12.

How to help: Ask your student how the poem fits the trees waking in Chapter 11.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What did Aslan mean when he said Lucy would find him bigger every year?Listen for: Aslan isn’t growing; Lucy is, and as she grows she understands more of him.
  2. Why was it hard for Lucy to wake the others?Listen for: they couldn’t see Aslan, and each time she told it, it sounded less convincing.
  3. What did Susan and Peter admit?Listen for: Susan had believed Lucy deep down but didn’t want to; Peter had been leading them wrong.
  4. What happened when Aslan roared?Listen for: the trees woke up and came toward him in human shapes, dancing.
  5. What did Nikabrik want to do, and why was it wrong?Listen for: call back the White Witch; she was cruel, and Caspian and the Badger refused.
  6. Which pillar did you see most this week?Listen for: a pillar and a real moment from Chapters 10–12.
