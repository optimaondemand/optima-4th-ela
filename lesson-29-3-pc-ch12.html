📌 Roots to Know

rupt = break / burst  |  duct / duce = lead  |  form = shape / change

Morphology connects to your spelling words: interrupt, disrupt, rupture, conduct, produce, introduce, transform, reform, uniform

Root: rupt (break / burst) — Choose all that make a real word:
✅ interrupt (inter + rupt), disrupt (dis + rupt), and rupture (rupt + ure) are all real words!
Root: duct / duce (lead) — Choose all that make a real word:
✅ conduct (con + duct), produce (pro + duce), and introduce (intro + duce) all work!
Root: form (shape / change) — Choose all that make a real word:
✅ transform (trans + form), reform (re + form), and uniform (uni + form) all work!

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“I’m a beast and we don’t change. I’m a badger, what’s more, and we hold on.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
Who says this? Trufflehunter the badger, pushing back against Nikabrik’s dark plan. What does he mean by “we don’t change”? He is saying that beasts are loyal — they stay true. And “we hold on” means they keep going even when things are hard. How does this contrast with what Nikabrik wants to do?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice the parallel structure: “I’m a beast and we don’t change. I’m a badger, what’s more, and we hold on.” Lewis uses the same pattern twice — “I’m a ___ and we ___.” The second sentence adds “what’s more,” which makes it feel even stronger, like Trufflehunter is building his argument. Why is repetition powerful here? What does it add to Trufflehunter’s character?
🖊️ Mimic
Write your own version using the same parallel pattern. Imagine a character (from any book or from your imagination) declaring who they are and what they do. Use the frame below.
