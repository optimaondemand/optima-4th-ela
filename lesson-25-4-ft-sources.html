✏ Grammar: Commas in a Series
Explain why they matter for clear writing.

When you list three or more items in a sentence, you use commas in a series (also called the serial comma or Oxford comma) to keep your ideas clear.

📌 Why Commas in a Series Matter

Without commas: “The soldiers destroyed cotton commissary stores and dwellings.” — Wait, is “cotton commissary stores” one thing or two? It’s confusing!

With commas: “The soldiers destroyed cotton, commissary stores, and dwellings.” — Now we see three separate items clearly.

Look at Source A from this week: the Wisconsin State Journal writer listed what was destroyed using commas in a series. Without those commas, the sentence would be a jumble.

🤔

Think about it: Read this sentence without commas and think about what could go wrong: “Harriet carried a lantern a pistol and medicine.” Is “a lantern a pistol” one item? Add the commas in your mind to see the three separate items.
