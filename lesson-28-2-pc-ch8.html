📚 What Good Readers Do
Your reading lens for today

Good readers notice the quiet conflicts — not just sword fights, but the hard choices. Today’s chapter doesn’t have a battle. But the children have to decide: walk or row? Who leads? Who keeps going when they’re exhausted?

Conflict isn’t always dramatic. Sometimes it’s just deciding what to do next when you’re tired and lost.

🧠 As you read today, ask yourself:

Notice the decisions the characters make. Who takes charge? Who pushes through? What does “leading” look like when there’s no battle to fight?

because the horn had summoned them the children knew they had to help caspian even though they were far from his army
Find: missing capital letters, missing comma after the introductory dependent clause.

Corrected: Because the Horn had summoned them, the children knew they had to help Caspian even though they were far from his army.

Capital B on “Because” (start of sentence). Capital H on “Horn” (specific important object). Capital C on “Caspian” (proper noun). Add a comma after “them” because the dependent clause comes first.

✏️ Grammar: Complex Sentences — Identify
Tap the dependent clause in each sentence.

A complex sentence has two parts: an independent clause (can stand alone) and a dependent clause (can’t stand alone — starts with words like because, although, when, even though). Tap the dependent clause in each sentence below.

Because the Horn had summoned them, the children knew they had to help. — The dependent clause is: “Because the Horn had summoned them.”
Although Trumpkin was doubtful, he agreed to lead them to Caspian.
Peter kept rowing even though his arms ached terribly.
When they passed the island, Susan remembered the Splendour Hyaline.
The children chose to row because the woods might be dangerous.
If Aslan wanted them in Narnia, Lucy believed they should be there.

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“Do you remember our voyage to Terebinthia… And the silken sails, and the great stern lanterns?… Do you remember when we had the musicians up in the rigging playing flutes so that it sounded like music out of the sky?”
📖 Read Like a Reader
What are Susan and Lucy doing? They’re remembering a beautiful voyage from their time as queens. Why do they bring up these memories now, in the middle of a hard, exhausting rowing trip?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice how Lewis uses questions — “Do you remember…” repeated. Each question adds a more beautiful detail: first the sails, then the lanterns, then the musicians playing flutes. Why does he structure it this way? What effect does the pattern create?
🖊️ Mimic
Write 2–3 sentences using the “Do you remember…” pattern to describe a memory. Each sentence should add a more vivid detail than the last.
