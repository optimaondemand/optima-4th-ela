Find: missing capital letters, missing commas.

Corrected: Although the group voted to go downstream, Lucy knew in her heart that Aslan had been real, so she followed even though it hurt.

Capital A on “Although” (start of sentence). Capital L on “Lucy” (proper noun). Capital A on “Aslan” (proper noun). Comma after “downstream” (dependent clause comes first). Comma before “so” (joins two clauses).

What I Saw
An Optima Original Poem
They voted, and the path was set. The downstream route, the safer bet. But I had seen the golden mane, The eyes that said, “This way again.” They didn’t see. They couldn’t know. And still I followed, walking slow, Because the thing that I had seen Was truer than the path between.

Teacher models explaining complex sentences
📋 Teacher Recording Notes
• Day 4 grammar = explain the concept
• Model: "A complex sentence has two parts..."
• Remind students: independent clause stands alone, dependent clause does not
• Encourage them to write like they’re talking to a 3rd grader
• ~2–3 minutes

You’ve spotted complex sentences and written your own. Now it’s time to explain them — as if a 3rd grader asked you, “What’s a complex sentence?”

💡

Write a short explanation (3–4 sentences) that a 3rd grader could understand. Include: what a complex sentence is, what makes a dependent clause different from an independent clause, and one example.

Practice first: tap the dependent clause in each sentence.
