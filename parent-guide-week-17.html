This Week at a Glance

In the middle of the night, Nazi officers pound on the Johansens’ door. Annemarie rips Ellen’s Star of David necklace off just before they see it, and Papa uses an old baby picture to explain Ellen’s dark hair. Then Mama takes the girls by train to Uncle Henrik’s house by the sea, where the grown-ups talk in code, and a casket appears for a great-aunt Annemarie has never heard of.

Reading skill
Explaining courage with evidence. Saying a character is brave isn’t enough. Your student points to what the character did, not just how they felt.
Big idea
Courage that has seconds, not minutes. Some bravery gets planned. Annemarie’s happens before she has time to think.

“She had imprinted the Star of David into her palm.”Narrator, Chapter 5

Words to Know

Use them at home!
intendto plan to do something on purpose
crediblebelievable; easy to trust
discreetcareful not to draw attention
tensetight with worry; nervous
Word parts
cred means “to believe.” tend means “to stretch or reach.”credible · credit · intend · extend
Spelling
Prefix and suffix review. Peel the parts off the front and back to find the base word underneath.un·friend·ly · resist·ance · danger·ous
Grammar
Pronoun-verb agreement. She, he, and it take a verb ending in -s. I, you, we, and they don’t.She watches. They wait.

Your Student’s Opinion Essay

This week the essay gets written. Your student learns the quote sandwich (a set-up sentence, a short quote from the book, then “so what?”), then writes an introduction with a hook and their claim, and two body paragraphs, each with a different reason and a different quote.

An example writer named Sam shows each step first with his own essay about Annemarie.

By Heart

Robins will wear their feathery fire
Whistling their whims on a low fence-wire;
And not one will know of the war, not one
Will care at last when it is done.Sara Teasdale, “There Will Come Soft Rains” (1920), lines 5–8

The same four lines from Teasdale’s poem, with a few more words fading on Days 2 and 4.

How to help: Say the first half of a line and let your student finish it.

💬

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What did Annemarie do when the soldiers came?Listen for: she pulled off Ellen’s necklace and held it so tightly the star pressed into her hand.
  2. How did Papa convince the soldier that Ellen was his daughter?Listen for: he showed baby pictures of Lise, who had dark hair as a baby.
  3. Why do the grown-ups talk in code?Listen for: the phone or other people might be listening; even “the weather for fishing” can carry a hidden message.
  4. Can someone be scared and brave at the same time?Listen for: any honest thinking. Courage is what you do while you’re scared.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Agreement by ear. “She and her sister was hiding” or “were hiding”? Say both out loud.
  • Dinner-table quote sandwich. Quote something a family member said today, then say what it shows.
  • Family code words. Make up a harmless code phrase, like Uncle Henrik’s fishing weather.
