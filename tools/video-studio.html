How to record a video

1. Set up (once)

  1. Open this page in Chrome and press Start camera. Choose Allow when Chrome asks.
  2. Pick your camera and microphone at the bottom right if the wrong ones show up.
  3. Face a window or lamp, with a plain wall behind you if you can.

2. Choose what you’re making

  • G4 shot list: pick a video on the left. It’s already named and knows where it goes. Tick the videos you want for a lesson, then press Skip the unticked so the rest stop showing up.
  • Free record: type a name for any video. It saves to your Downloads folder.
  • Just me or Screen + me: Screen + me shows a tab or window with you in a corner.

Best quality or Live?

  • ✨ Best quality (the usual choice) records your camera as it is, sharp and smooth, at 1080p when your camera can. While you record, the studio notes what you put on screen and when. After you press Keep it, the take finishes in the background: the background you pick under 🎨 Finished look goes in, every card comes back at its moment, and the trim is applied. You can record the next video while it finishes. Finishing pauses while you record.
  • Takes are saved in this browser the moment you stop, so closing the tab doesn’t lose them. They pick up again next time you open the studio.
  • 🎞 Re-finish this video opens the clean original again (kept for 3 weeks, or longer in a folder you pick) so you can change the background, trim or cards without recording again.
  • ⚡ Live records exactly what you see. Use it for Screen + me, or for a quick take.
  • 💡 Check my light (Camera and mic) tells you if you need more light. Good light makes the biggest difference to how sharp you look.

3. Record

  • Press ● Record or Space. A 3-second countdown runs first.
  • P pauses and resumes. It all stays one video.
  • ✏ Draw (or D) lets you circle or underline things. Ink fades on its own unless you untick Fade.
  • Quotes: when your script says Quote on screen, click the gold line in the script (or press 6) and the quote appears on a band across the bottom of the video. Click again when you’ve read it. ＋ Type a quote adds your own.
  • Words: this lesson’s vocabulary words are under the video. Click one to show its card, click again to take it off. 5 steps through them.
  • This part (7): what the section you’re filming can show, pulled from the lesson: the DOL sentence on a sentence strip (then ✓ Show the fix), word parts, the grammar or spelling rule, the reading move, Pause & Think stops, chapter questions, and the writing focus. Vocabulary words in your script are underlined; click one to show its card.
  • On-screen cues in a script: a gold 🎬 On screen row inside the script is a suggestion for that moment. Click a button in it (a DOL sentence, ✓ Show the fix, a rule, a chapter question, the chapter picture, a word card or confetti) and it goes up on the video, the same as the On screen buttons beside the script.
  • Chapter picture (8): the lesson’s painting slides in beside you as a small framed card.
  • Confetti: 1.
  • Press ■ Stop or Space when you’re done.

4. Check it

  • Trim: drag the handles to cut the start and end.
  • Captions: fix any wrong words. Click a time to jump there.
  • Pick a thumbnail, then Keep it or Download now.
  • ❓ Stop and answer: play to a moment, pause, press ＋ Question here. On the lesson page the video stops there until the student answers (multiple choice, a written answer, or just a pause to think). The 💡 Ideas row suggests ready-made questions from the lesson: its chapter questions, Pause & Think stops, “which word means…” for the vocabulary, and word-part meanings. Already recorded a video? Pick it and press ❓ Add questions or captions to this video.

Your privacy

Your video never leaves this computer. It stays in this tab until you download or save it. The background tool downloads a small model from Google the first time, but nothing is sent back. The one exception is Make captions while I record: Chrome sends your audio to Google to turn speech into words. Leave it off and use Build from my script instead.

If something goes wrong

  • Choppy video or a slow computer? The studio switches to the Faster background on its own. Turning the background to None helps most.
  • No camera? Click the camera icon at the right end of Chrome’s address bar and choose Allow.
  • Closed the tab by mistake? Takes you didn’t download are gone, so download as you go.
