📚 What Good Readers Do
Your reading lens for today

Good readers notice tension. C.S. Lewis builds suspense not just through action, but through small details — a pause, a description, a choice a character makes when no one is watching.

In Chapter 14, pay attention to how Lewis makes you feel nervous during the combat scene. Notice the details that slow down time and the choices that reveal character.

🧠 As you read today, ask yourself:

What details create suspense during Peter’s fight with Miraz? When does the tension feel highest? And what do Peter’s choices during the fight reveal about who he is?

✏️ Grammar: Dialogue Punctuation — Identify
Spot whether dialogue punctuation is correct or incorrect in each sentence.
📌 Dialogue Punctuation Rules

Quotation marks go around the spoken words. “I accept your challenge,” said Peter.

Comma or period goes inside the closing quotation mark when a speaker tag follows. “Stand back,” he said.

Capital letter begins every new piece of speech. Edmund said, “He is coming.”

End punctuation (period, ?, !) goes inside the closing quotation mark. “Treachery!” they cried.

Read each sentence. Tap CORRECT if the dialogue punctuation is right, or INCORRECT if something is wrong.

✅ CORRECT ❌ INCORRECT

📌 What Was Wrong in the Incorrect Ones?

Sentence 2: Missing capital T on “Treachery” (start of speech), missing closing quotation mark and exclamation mark.
Sentence 3: Missing comma after “whispered” before the opening quotation mark.

⏸️ Pause & Think #1

Peter tells Edmund, “Give my love to everyone at home, Ed, if he gets me.” He says this quietly, before the combat resumes. Why does Peter say this now? What does this moment tell you about what Peter believes might happen — and what kind of person says something like that before a fight?

Miraz trips on a tussock of grass. Peter steps back instead of striking. Edmund thinks “Need he be as gentlemanly as all that?” Why does Peter step back? What does it cost him? And what does that choice say about the difference between winning and fighting with honor?

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“Grabbed Miraz’s arm as it came down,” said Trumpkin, dancing with delight. “There’s a man for you! Uses his enemy’s arm as a ladder.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
What is Trumpkin describing? He’s retelling a moment from the combat — excitedly, in fragments. Why does he speak in a fragment (“Grabbed Miraz’s arm”) instead of a complete sentence? What does that tell you about how he’s feeling right now?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Lewis uses an action fragment as dialogue, then a speaker tag with action (“said Trumpkin, dancing with delight”), then Trumpkin’s excited commentary. Notice how the fragment creates energy — it feels rushed and thrilled. Notice also the comma after “Trumpkin” that links his action to his speech. How does this pattern make you feel the excitement?
🖊️ Mimic
Write your own version using this pattern: an action fragment as dialogue, then a speaker tag with movement, then excited commentary. It can be about any character — from this book or your own imagination.
“[Action fragment],” said [character], [action verb with delight]. “[Excited comment]!”
