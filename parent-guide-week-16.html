This Week at a Glance

The novel begins. It’s September 1943 in Copenhagen, Denmark, three years into the German occupation your student read about last week. Ten-year-old Annemarie Johansen, her little sister Kirsti, and Annemarie’s Jewish best friend, Ellen Rosen, race home from school and are stopped by German soldiers on the corner. By the end of Chapter 4, the Nazis have begun rounding up Denmark’s Jewish families, and Ellen spends the night with the Johansens, pretending to be Annemarie’s sister.

💛 A heads-up for families
Annemarie’s older sister, Lise, died before the story begins, and the family still misses her. She comes up often in these chapters. The novel is gentle about it, but some students may want to talk about it.
Reading skill
Inferring danger from small details. A padlocked shop, a careful answer, a parent’s worried face: small clues tell the reader something is wrong before anyone says it.
Big idea
Small, ordinary courage. Sometimes courage looks like a ten-year-old answering a soldier politely, and then stopping herself from saying too much.

“Friends will take care of them. That’s what friends do.”Mama, Chapter 3

Words to Know

Use them at home!
interruptto stop something while it is happening
conducthow someone behaves; or, to lead or guide
relocateto move someone or something to a new place, sometimes by force
suspiciona feeling that something is wrong, even without proof
Word parts
rupt means “to break.” duct and duce mean “to lead.”interrupt · erupt · conduct · introduce
Spelling
The schwa sound in history words: an unstressed vowel that just says “uh.” Trick: find a related word where you can hear it.separate → separation · soldier · government
Grammar
Possessive pronouns show who owns something, and they never take an apostrophe.The necklace was hers. Its chain broke.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did the soldiers stop Annemarie and Ellen?Listen for: they were running, which made the soldiers suspicious; Annemarie answered politely and kept it short.
  2. What did the Danish man say about the king’s bodyguard?Listen for: “All of Denmark is his bodyguard.” The people themselves protect their king.
  3. What happened to Mrs. Hirsch’s button shop?Listen for: it was padlocked with a German sign; the Hirsches are Jewish, and the Nazis were closing Jewish businesses.
  4. Why is Ellen staying with the Johansens?Listen for: the Nazis planned to take Jewish families that night, so Ellen pretends to be Annemarie’s sister.

Try at Home

5 minutes

  • Find Copenhagen on a map, then find Sweden just across the water.
  • Stay-calm stories. Share a time you had to stay calm when you were scared.
