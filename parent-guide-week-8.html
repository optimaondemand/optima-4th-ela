This Week at a Glance

Marilla takes Anne back to Mrs. Spencer, meaning to send her away, and changes her mind. Anne gets to stay. Then her bringing-up begins: her first prayer, her first chores, and her first meeting with Mrs. Rachel Lynde, which ends with Anne stamping her foot and shouting. By Friday she has made a very dramatic apology and, for the first time in her life, calls a place home.

Reading skill
Tracking how a character begins to change. Your student watches Marilla soften and Anne start to belong.
Growing-up theme
Belonging. Also: learning what nobody taught you, and the difference between having a temper and having a reason.

“It’s lovely to be going home and know it’s home.”Anne Shirley, Chapter 10

Words to Know

Use them at home!
transfiguredchanged in how someone looks, especially in a glowing, lit-up way
benevolentkindly; meaning well; gentle in spirit
irresistibletoo tempting or strong to resist
penitentfeeling truly sorry for doing something wrong
Word parts
sub- means “under.” inter- means “between.”submarine · submerge · interrupt · international
Spelling
Plural or possessive? They sound the same. Plurals never take an apostrophe; possessives always do.two windows · the window’s frame
Grammar
Possessive nouns. One owner: add ’s. More than one owner ending in s: add just ’.Anne’s trunk · the girls’ room

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Marilla was going to send Anne away. What made her change her mind?Listen for: Mrs. Blewett seemed hard and unkind, and Marilla saw the look on Anne’s face.
  2. Why do you think Anne made up imaginary friends like Katie Maurice?Listen for: she was lonely and had no real family or friends who stayed.
  3. Mrs. Rachel was rude, and Anne was rude back. Who was in the wrong?Listen for: both. Marilla scolds Anne but also tells Mrs. Rachel she was too hard on her.
  4. How is your story’s main character different at the end than at the start?Listen for: a clear before and after, like “scared to try” becoming “willing to try.”

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Apostrophe hunt. Look at signs, labels, or a menu. Does each word mean more than one or belongs to?
  • Who owns it? Name things around the house with possessives: Dad’s mug, the dogs’ bowls.
  • Talk about belonging. Where does your family feel most at home? Anne would love the question.
