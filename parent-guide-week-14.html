🧭 How the Lesson Pages Work

  • 🗂️Four tabs, in order: Warm-Up → Word Study → Reading → Assignment. A button at the bottom of each tab moves to the next.
  • 💾Answers save as you type, but only in that browser on that computer. Switching computers or clearing the browser loses them.
  • 📋Everything is gathered at the end. The bottom of the Assignment tab collects every answer from the lesson in one place.
  • 📄Download as PDF saves that work as a “My Work” journal page. Use it (or Copy) to submit in Canvas. A lesson isn’t turned in until it’s submitted there.
  • 🔊Listen at the top reads the page aloud, with a speed control. Check My Work and hint buttons give instant feedback.
  • 🎙️Fluency is a separate page in the Canvas module, because recording doesn’t work inside a lesson. Keep the novel and a paper reading journal nearby too.

This Week at a Glance

The last four chapters. At Queen’s, Anne and Gilbert are still rivals, but friendly ones now. Anne wins a scholarship to college, and Matthew tells her how proud he is. Then Matthew dies suddenly. Marilla’s eyesight is failing and she may have to sell Green Gables, so Anne gives up her scholarship to stay home and teach. Gilbert quietly gives her the Avonlea school, and the two finally become friends. Your student also publishes their finished story this week.

💛 A heads-up for families
Matthew dies in Chapter 37 (Day 3). Many readers find it the hardest chapter in the book, and some students may be sad or want to talk. You might read it together, or ask afterward how they felt. The book shows grief gently: Anne can’t cry at first, then she can, and Marilla comforts her in the night.
Reading skill
How a character changes across a whole novel. Your student compares the orphan at the train station with the young teacher at the end.
Growing-up theme
The bend in the road. Plans change, and growing up means facing what comes next with hope.

“I don’t know what lies around the bend, but I’m going to believe that the best does.”Anne Shirley, Chapter 38

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Matthew said he’d rather have Anne than a dozen boys. Why is that line so special?Listen for: at the very start of the book, the Cuthberts wanted a boy, not Anne.
  2. How did you feel when Matthew died? How did Anne and Marilla help each other?Listen for: any honest feeling. Marilla comforts Anne at night and finally says she loves her.
  3. Why did Anne give up her scholarship? Was it the right choice?Listen for: to stay with Marilla and keep Green Gables. Either opinion is fine if they give a reason.
  4. Can you read me your finished story? What part are you proudest of?Listen for: a specific part, and maybe something they’d still change.

The Anne Scrapbook

End-of-unit project

After Lesson 14.4, your student finishes the book with Anne’s Growing-Up Scrapbook, a seven-page scrapbook that shows how Anne changes: Anne when she arrives, three turning points, Anne at the end, a map of Avonlea, and a cover with one sentence about what growing up means. They choose pictures and real lines from the book, write a little on each page, and then save it as one PDF to turn in on Canvas. Plan on one sitting.

How to help: ask your student to walk you through their three turning points and why they chose them. The work saves only in the browser they use, so they should finish on the same computer. Keep a copy of the PDF. It’s theirs to keep.
