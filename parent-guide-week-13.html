  • 🗂️Four tabs, in order: Warm-Up → Word Study → Reading → Assignment. A button at the bottom of each tab moves to the next.
  • 💾Answers save as you type, but only in that browser on that computer. Switching computers or clearing the browser loses them.
  • 📋Everything is gathered at the end. The bottom of the Assignment tab collects every answer from the lesson in one place.

This Week at a Glance

Anne is a teenager now. She grows taller and quieter, and Marilla realizes the little girl is gone. Anne passes the entrance exam for Queen’s Academy tied for first place with Gilbert, and the person she most wants to make proud is Matthew. She beats stage fright at a hotel concert, then leaves home for Queen’s, promising Marilla she is still the same Anne inside. This is also the week your student edits their story.

Reading skill
Stated and implied theme. Some life lessons a character says out loud. Others the story shows through what happens.
Growing-up theme
Knowing who you are. Also: noticing your own change, who you want to tell first, and deciding what counts as rich.

“I’m not a bit changed—not really. I’m only just pruned down and branched out.”Anne Shirley, Chapter 34

Words to Know

Use them at home!
composurestaying calm and steady
ambitiona strong desire to achieve something important
broadmindedopen to new ideas and other points of view
tactfulcareful not to hurt other people’s feelings
Word roots
spect means “look.” struct means “build.”inspect · respect · construct · instruct
Spelling
-er and -est. Short words add the ending; long words use more and most. Consonant + y becomes i.taller · happiest · more beautiful
Grammar
Linking verbs don’t show action. They link the subject to a description.Anne was proud · she seemed calm

By Heart: This Unit’s Poem

Who has seen the wind?
Neither I nor you:
But when the leaves hang trembling,
The wind is passing through.Christina Rossetti

This is the poem your student is memorizing across the Anne unit. It comes up on Days 2 and 4, and the unit ends next week.

How to help: Ask your student to recite the whole poem for you, maybe for another family member too. If they joined this week, that’s fine. The poem starts from the full text for every new student.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How has Anne changed since she first came to Green Gables? How is she the same?Listen for: taller, quieter, fewer big words, but still imaginative and loving.
  2. When Anne passed the exam, who did she most want to tell, and why?Listen for: Matthew, because she wanted to make him proud.
  3. Anne says she and her friends are already rich. What does she mean?Listen for: youth, happiness, imagination, and beauty around them, not diamonds or money.
  4. Read me one line of dialogue from your story. Where do the quotation marks and commas go?Listen for: marks around the spoken words, and a comma before “she said.”

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Big, bigger, biggest. Compare three things at home: tall, taller, tallest; funny, funnier, funniest.
  • What makes us rich? Like Anne, name things your family has that money can’t buy.
  • Then and now. Look at an old photo together. How has your student changed, and what’s still the same?
