🧭 How the Lesson Pages Work

  • 🗂️Four tabs, in order: Warm-Up → Word Study → Reading → Assignment. A button at the bottom of each tab moves to the next.
  • 💾Answers save as you type, but only in that browser on that computer. Switching computers or clearing the browser loses them.
  • 📋Everything is gathered at the end. The bottom of the Assignment tab collects every answer from the lesson in one place.
  • 📄Download as PDF saves that work as a “My Work” journal page. Use it (or Copy) to submit in Canvas. A lesson isn’t turned in until it’s submitted there.
  • 🔊Listen at the top reads the page aloud, with a speed control. Check My Work and hint buttons give instant feedback.
  • 🎙️Fluency is a separate page in the Canvas module, because recording doesn’t work inside a lesson. Keep the novel and a paper reading journal nearby too.

This Week at a Glance

Anne and her friends start a Story Club. She buys hair dye from a peddler who promises it will turn her hair black, and it turns green instead. Playing a sad poem on the pond, her boat leaks, and Gilbert Blythe rescues her (she still won’t be his friend). Then she visits the city with Diana, decides she likes home best, and joins Miss Stacy’s class to prepare for the entrance exam to Queen’s Academy, the teachers’ college. This is also the week your student revises their story.

Reading skill
Summarizing. Your student practices telling the important events in a sentence or two, without retelling everything.
Growing-up theme
Learning from mistakes. Anne starts naming what each of her disasters has taught her.

“Dyeing my hair cured me of vanity.”Anne Shirley, Chapter 28

Words to Know

Use them at home!
diligenthard-working and careful
vanitytoo much pride in how you look
implicitlywithout questioning; trusting completely
epocha memorable period of time; a turning point
Word roots
port means “carry.” tract means “pull.”transport · portable · attract · distract
Spelling
Keep the e after ce/ge before a suffix starting with a or o, so the c or g stays soft.courageous · manageable · changing
Grammar
Irregular past-tense verbs. Some change a vowel, some become a new word, some don’t change at all.sang · went · put

By Heart: This Unit’s Poem

Who has seen the wind?
Neither I nor you:
But when the leaves hang trembling,
The wind is passing through.Christina Rossetti

This is the poem your student is memorizing across the Anne unit. It comes up on Days 2 and 4, and by now most of the words have faded from the screen.

How to help: Ask your student to say the whole poem to you from memory. If they joined this week, that’s fine. The poem starts from the full text for every new student.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did Anne’s hair end up green?Listen for: a peddler promised the dye would make it black, Anne believed him, and Marilla had to cut her hair short.
  2. What happened on the pond, and why wouldn’t Anne be friends with Gilbert afterward?Listen for: the boat leaked, Gilbert rescued her, and she was still angry about “Carrots.”
  3. Can you tell me everything that happened this week in just two sentences?Listen for: the biggest events only, not every detail. That’s this week’s reading skill.
  4. What did you change in your story this week, and why?Listen for: something added, cut, moved, or swapped for a better word.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Movie in two sentences. Summarize a show or movie you watched in two sentences. What did you leave out?
  • Tricky past tense. Take turns: go → went, sing → sang, bring → brought, cut → cut.
  • My mistake taught me… Like Anne, share one mistake and what it taught you.
