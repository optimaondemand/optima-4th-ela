🌱 Morphology: Word Bases port, tract, struct
Latin word bases that build big words

Many English words are built from Latin word bases. When you know the base, you can figure out words you’ve never seen before.

📌 Key Word Bases

port = carry  |  tract = pull / draw  |  struct = build

Example words: transport (carry across), portable (able to be carried), extract (pull out), tractor (something that pulls), construct (build together), structure (something that is built).

✏️ Grammar: Conjunctions (but / so)
Define the skill

Conjunctions are words that connect ideas. “But” shows contrast — two ideas that push against each other. “So” shows result — one thing leads to another.

“Caspian loved the old stories, but his uncle said they were nonsense.”
“Miraz was furious about the Nurse’s tales, so he sent her away.”

In the first sentence, “but” shows the contrast between Caspian’s love and Miraz’s disapproval. In the second, “so” shows the result — the Nurse was dismissed because Miraz was angry.

🔠 Spelling: Suffix Rules in Multisyllabic Words
Discover the pattern

Look at how adding a suffix changes the base word. What patterns do you notice?

Group A — -tion / -ous

educate → education

converse → conversation

danger → dangerous

Group B — -ful / -ness / -ly

wonderful

mysterious

happiness

🔍 What do you notice?

Look at the base words and their suffixed forms. Does the base word stay the same, or does something change? What happens to the “e” in “educate”? What happens to the “y” in “happy”?

When a word ends in silent e, drop the e before adding a suffix that starts with a vowel: educate → education, converse → conversation.

When a word ends in consonant + y, change the y to i before adding a suffix: happy → happiness, mystery → mysterious.

When a suffix starts with a consonant (-ful, -ly, -ness), the base word usually stays the same: wonder → wonderful, danger → dangerous.

Knowing these rules helps you spell long words correctly — you just need to know the base word and the suffix.
