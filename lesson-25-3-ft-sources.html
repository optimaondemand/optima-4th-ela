🌱 Morphology: Prefix Review
trans- · inter- · sub- · super- — direction and position prefixes

These four prefixes tell you about direction or position. Knowing them helps you decode unfamiliar words instantly.

📌 Prefix Meanings

trans- = across (transport, translate)  |  inter- = between (interview, international)  |  sub- = under/below (submarine, subtitle)  |  super- = above/beyond (superhero, supernatural)

✏️ Grammar: Commas in a Series
Day 3 — Write your own sentences using commas in a series.

When you list three or more items in a sentence, you separate them with commas. Place a comma after each item and use and or or before the last one.

The raid destroyed cotton, commissary stores, and lordly dwellings.
Three items in the series: cotton, commissary stores, and lordly dwellings. Each item is separated by a comma.
✍️

Your turn: Write 3 original sentences about Harriet Tubman or the Combahee River Raid. Each sentence must include a comma-separated series of at least three items. Write in your reading journal.

💡

Try listing actions (verbs), people (nouns), or descriptions (adjectives) in your series. Example starters: “Tubman guided, protected, and...” or “The raid freed men, women, and...”

🔠 Spelling: Frequently Confused Words
Day 3 — Show What You Know (today’s graded response)

These word pairs sound alike but mean different things. Getting them right shows careful, polished writing.

their / there / they’re

their = belonging to them
there = a place
they’re = they are

its / it’s

its = belonging to it
it’s = it is / it has

your / you’re

your = belonging to you
you’re = you are

📜 Open Your Sources
Review all three accounts of the Combahee River Raid
📰 Source A — Wisconsin State Journal (1863)

Type: Primary source  |  Author: Anonymous reporter  |  Key detail: Never named Tubman — called her “She-Moses”

“Montgomery’s gallant band of 300 soldiers under the guidance of a Black woman, dashed into the enemies’ country, struck a bold and effective blow, destroying millions of dollars worth of commissary store, cotton and lordly dwellings, and striking terror to the heart of the rebeldom brought off near 800 slaves and thousands of dollars worth of property, without losing a man or receiving a scratch.”
📰 Source B — Franklin Sanborn, The Commonwealth (1863)

Type: Primary source  |  Author: Franklin Sanborn  |  Key detail: Named Tubman and included her own dictated words

“We weakened the rebels somewhat on the Combahee River by taking and bringing away seven hundred and fifty-six head of their most valuable livestock, known up in your region as ‘contrabands,’ and this, too, without the loss of a single life on our part, though we had good reasons to believe that a number of rebels bit the dust.”
📕 Source C — Sarah Bradford, Scenes in the Life of Harriet Tubman (1869)

Type: Secondary source  |  Author: Sarah Bradford  |  Key detail: Written 6 years after the raid, compiled from letters, newspaper articles, and interviews. Authorized by Tubman.

Bradford’s biography drew from multiple sources — including Tubman’s own stories — to create a fuller, more detailed picture. Because it was written later, Bradford could include context and reflection that the newspaper reporters did not.

🔎 Key Differences to Notice

Naming: Source A calls her “a Black woman” — Source B names her directly. Voice: Source B includes Tubman’s own words; Sources A and C describe her from the outside. Tone: Source A is dramatic and newspaper-style; Source B is ironic and pointed; Source C is detailed and biographical. Numbers: Source A says “near 800”; Source B gives the exact number: 756.
