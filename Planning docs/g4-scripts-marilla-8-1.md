## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-welcome.mp3` — Warm-Up (in the greeting)

> Marilla Cuthbert again. I’ve the buggy out and that child beside me, and we’re bound for White Sands to have Mrs. Spencer’s mistake put right. Today’s question, I’m told, is what I might see in her that would change my mind. Well, my mind is made up, and I don’t change it lightly. Read, and judge for yourself. We’ll also sort out the little mark that shows a thing belongs to somebody.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-wgrd.mp3` — Warm-Up · What Good Readers Do

> People don’t change all at once. I certainly don’t. If a person changes at all, it starts small: a pause, a second thought, a thing done that doesn’t match what was said. Today you’re to watch me, of all people. Write down what I mean to do at the start. Then mark the very moment I stop and think, and ask yourself what I’d just seen.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-dol.mp3` — Warm-Up · Daily Oral Language

> Something in this sentence belongs to somebody, and it wants its proper mark to say so. There’s a place named in it, too, and a place deserves its capitals the same as a person does. Look at the model first and see how it’s done. Then set the second sentence to rights. Neatly, mind. A sentence is like a kitchen. Everything belongs in its place.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-guide1.mp3` — Word Study tab

> Now then, word study. Two word parts this week, and both of them tell you where a thing stands. One means under. The other means between. I find myself between two things just now, a mistake I didn’t make and a child I didn’t send for. So I’ll be glad of some plain, orderly work. Take the words one at a time, and check as you go.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-morphology.mp3` — Word Study · Morphology

> Every word here begins with one of the two parts, the one for under or the one for between. Cover the front of the word with your finger and look at what’s left. Quite often it’s a word you know already. Then put the front part back and ask what it adds. Does the meaning sit under something, or between things? Decide that first, and the spelling will follow.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-vocabulary.mp3` — Word Study · Vocabulary

> Each of these words comes with its own sentence from the book. Read that sentence right through before you so much as touch the card. Then look inside the word for any part you know. Only then turn it over and check. Guess first and check after, and a word stays with you. Flip the cards as fast as you can, and it’s gone again like a hen out of the yard.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-grammar.mp3` — Word Study · Grammar

> You’re to sort these without being told what the groups are. I like that. It makes a person think. In every one, something belongs to somebody. The question is how many somebodies. Look hard at where the little mark sits. Say them aloud, and you’ll find they sound exactly alike. So your eyes will have to do the work your ears can’t.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-spelling.mp3` — Word Study · Spelling

> More than one, or belonging to? That’s today’s question, and it matters more than people think. I’ve seen grown people get it wrong on a shop sign. Before you drop a word into its column, ask whether anything in it belongs to anybody. Then decide. Don’t go sprinkling those little marks about like salt, just to be safe. A mark in the wrong place is worse than none.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-copia.mp3` — Word Study · Say It Three Ways

> Here’s a plain sentence about where Mrs. Spencer lives, and you’re to say it three ways. Put a when or a where at the front of it. Swap the doing word for one that fits better. Then tell us more about the where or the how. Change one thing at a time, and keep it true. I’ll be at that yellow house soon enough myself, so mind you describe it properly.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-guide2.mp3` — Reading tab

> White Sands at last. Mrs. Spencer will sort this out, and the child will go where she was meant to go. This week’s word is belonging, and I’ll say this much: a person belongs where there’s a proper use for them. That’s plain sense. As you read, watch the child’s face whenever somebody talks about where she’s to go. Faces tell more than people mean them to.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-read.mp3` — Reading · Read: Ch. 6

> There are two places to stop. The first comes partway through, just as something is about to be settled. The second comes at the very end. Stop at both, even if you’d rather hurry on. And at the second, don’t expect a speech from me. I’ve never been one for speeches. Ask yourself why a person might keep their feelings close, and whether that means they haven’t got any.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-guide3.mp3` — Assignment tab

> Well. I went to White Sands to send her back, and I came home with her still beside me. Mrs. Blewett would have taken her, and I’d heard what she’s like to work for. Then I saw the child’s face. I told Matthew she may stay. It seems a sort of duty, and I’ll leave it at that. Now it’s your turn. Say what you saw, and prove it from the book.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-chapter.mp3` — Assignment · Chapter Thinking

> Three questions, and the last is about me, so be fair. For the first, I had my reasons for hesitating. One of them I’d heard, and one I saw with my own eyes. For the second, you’ll need the author’s own words about that face, two of them, copied exactly. As for the third, I said it was duty. Whether that’s the whole of it, I leave to you.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-wordconn.mp3` — Assignment · Word Study Connection

> Your word-part answer is saved, so here’s how it fits. All week you’ve had one part for under and one for between. Well, I found myself standing between that child and Mrs. Blewett, and I didn’t care for it. And I couldn’t picture her under that woman’s roof. A word part tells you where a thing stands. What you do tells people where you stand.

## `marilla\g4ela-8-1-workshop.mp3` — Assignment · Writer’s Workshop

> You’re building a character today, with one thing she wants and one thing she’s afraid of. Make them real, not just feeling words. That child never told me she was afraid of Mrs. Blewett. She sat perfectly still and stared at her, and her face was pale as anything. That told me plenty. Give your character a want and a fear as plain to see as that.
