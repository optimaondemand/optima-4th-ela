## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-welcome.mp3` — Warm-Up (in the greeting)

> Hello, it’s Matthew Cuthbert again. I’ll be with you today. Here’s the question. By the end of this chapter somebody calls Green Gables home, and you’re to work out what had to happen first. It’s the last day of the week, so you’ll look back at how far somebody’s come. And you’ll play editor, finding apostrophes that went astray. Take it slow. That’s how I do things.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-dol.mp3` — Warm-Up · Daily Oral Language

> Here’s how I’d go at it. Copy the sentence out first, just as it is. Then read the model aloud, slowly, and listen for where your voice pauses and where it stops. Now look at the model again and see what the writer put in those spots. Do the same in yours. Last of all, look at the names. The model will show you what they need.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide1.mp3` — Word Study tab

> Four jobs today, and one of them you get to choose. Take them one box at a time. If one goes wrong, it’s no great trouble. You can always try again. I never got anything right the first time myself, not even driving the mare. After the words there are two drafts to fix, like a careful editor. Read those slowly, out loud if you can.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-morphology.mp3` — Word Study · Morphology

> You’ve matched these before, so this is just a check. If you get one wrong, it’s no trouble. Leave it, do the others, and come back when there are only two left. You’ll have a fair chance then. If you’re properly stuck, think of a long word you know that uses the piece, and say that word out loud. The meaning’s usually sitting right in it.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-vocabulary.mp3` — Word Study · Vocabulary

> If you can’t decide which to pick, I’d try explaining a word to somebody younger. Picture a little one sitting beside you. Say it out loud first, in plain words, before you write anything. If you can make a little one understand it, you understand it yourself. Whatever you choose, use the word in a sentence about a real feeling.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-grammar.mp3` — Word Study · Grammar

> Read each sentence out loud, slowly. When you come to a word with an apostrophe, stop and ask what it’s doing there. Does somebody own something? And when a word owns something, check that it has its apostrophe. Don’t tap every sentence with an apostrophe in it. Some of them are right just as they are.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-spelling.mp3` — Word Study · Spelling

> Same sort of job, different draft. This time, some sentences have an apostrophe on a word that only means more than one. Those need tapping too. For each sentence, ask yourself one thing. Is this word just more than one of something, or does it own something? That question settles most of them. Take your time over it. There’s no prize for finishing first.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide2.mp3` — Reading tab

> Anne hasn’t come down, and Marilla won’t say why. I’ve been hanging about the kitchen like a lost calf. This week’s watch is belonging. If you want a way to keep track of it, notice every time somebody does a small kind thing without being asked. I’d keep a count. Belonging doesn’t come with a speech. It comes in little things like that.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-wgrd.mp3` — Reading · What Good Readers Do

> Before you read, take a moment and picture Anne at White Sands, sitting quiet while grown-ups decided where she’d go. Hold that picture. Then as you read today, watch for one small thing she does that the girl at White Sands couldn’t have done. When you find it, you’ve measured how far she’s come. And watch whoever’s standing next to her. They may have moved too.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-read.mp3` — Reading · Read: Ch. 10

> There’s one stop, at the very end, so you can read straight through. Some of this chapter is funny, and you’re allowed to laugh. When you reach the end, though, stop before you turn to the questions. Read the stopping question and sit with it for a bit. Think about what one small thing might mean to somebody who’s never had it.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide3.mp3` — Assignment tab

> Well, now you know. I went upstairs. I hadn’t been up there in four years, not since I helped Marilla paper the spare bedroom. I asked Anne if she’d just smooth it over, and she said she’d do anything for me. Don’t tell Marilla I had a hand in it. Now, go and find your answers in the book. Take your time. I’ll wait.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-chapter.mp3` — Assignment · Chapter Thinking

> For the first question, think about what I’m like with people, and what it cost me to climb those stairs. That’s your clue. For the second, find the part where Anne says sorry and read it again. Notice how she says it, not just what. For the last one, look back over what you wrote earlier this week. You may already have the moment written down.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-wordconn.mp3` — Assignment · Word Study Connection

> Your vocabulary answer is saved, and it’ll show up with your work. If you’ve got a moment, read it over once. Here’s something to think on. One of this week’s words was penitent, somebody who’s truly sorry. Anne said sorry to Rachel, and Rachel forgave her. Whether Anne was penitent, or just enjoying herself a good deal, I’ll leave to you.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-workshop.mp3` — Assignment · Writer’s Workshop

> Here’s how I’d start, if it were me. Write one line about how your character feels at the very beginning of your story. Then write one line about how they feel at the very end. Now you know the shape. The story is just how they get from one line to the other. Anne came a long way this week. Your character needn’t go so far.

---

**How he stays in character.** Shy, gentle, a man of few words, who shows how he feels by what he does.

**What he knows, and when.** Before the reading he doesn’t know what happened with Mrs. Rachel. Marilla only tells him at the start of Ch. 10. He just knows Anne hasn’t come down and Marilla won’t say why. Going upstairs, "smooth it over" and "don’t tell Marilla" come out only in the Assignment-tab clips. He wasn’t at Mrs. Rachel’s or on the walk home, so he never describes those scenes as if he saw them.

**Checked against the novel.** Everything he mentions is in Ch. 6–10: Marilla driving Anne to White Sands, not having been upstairs in his own house for four years (since he helped paper the spare bedroom), "just smooth it over", "I’d do anything for you", "don’t tell Marilla" and "putting my oar in", and Mrs. Rachel forgiving Anne. No clip reads the page, says a word part on its own, gives away an answer on the page, or names a time of day. Every clip is 40–75 words.
