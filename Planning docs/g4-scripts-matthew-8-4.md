## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-welcome.mp3` — Warm-Up (in the greeting)

> Hello, it’s Matthew Cuthbert again. I’ll be guiding you today. Here’s the question. By the end of this chapter somebody calls Green Gables home, and you’re to work out what had to happen first for her to feel that way. You’ll look back over the whole week and see how far somebody’s come. And you get to play editor, hunting down apostrophes that went astray.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-dol.mp3` — Warm-Up · Daily Oral Language

> Somebody’s talking again in this model, so look it over slowly. There are two little words squeezed short, each with its mark, and a pause just before the talking stops. Then you find out who said it. The one you’re fixing has nearly all the same pieces, and a couple of names that want standing up tall. Take your time. There’s no hurry.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide1.mp3` — Word Study tab

> Well now, it’s the last day of the week. You’ve got the same word parts you’ve been working with, and then a choice about what to do with the week’s words. I like that. I’m not much for being told exactly what to do myself. Pick whichever suits you and do it properly. After that there are two drafts to check over, the way a careful editor would.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-morphology.mp3` — Word Study · Morphology

> You’ve matched these before, so this is a check on whether they stuck. Don’t hurry it. If one gives you pause, think of a longer word you know that has that piece in it, and say it out loud. Most times the meaning’s sitting right there, waiting on you. That’s how I learned my farm tools. By using them, not by reading about them.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-vocabulary.mp3` — Word Study · Vocabulary

> Four words, and you get to pick what to do with them. If you want my advice, choose the one that makes you think a bit. Explaining a word to somebody younger is harder than it sounds. Whatever you choose, use the word the way the book does, in a real sentence about a real feeling. That’s how a word becomes yours.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-grammar.mp3` — Word Study · Grammar

> Somebody wrote a draft about the chapter, and some of the apostrophes are fine and some aren’t. Don’t go tapping everything with a mark. Read each sentence as if it were yours and you were about to hand it to Marilla. Would it pass? A sentence might be missing a mark it ought to have, or have one it shouldn’t.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-spelling.mp3` — Word Study · Spelling

> Same sort of job, different draft. Tap only the sentences where a mark is in the wrong place, or missing where it’s wanted. For each one, ask yourself whether the word is just more than one of something, or whether it owns something. That question settles most of them. Take your time over it. There’s no prize for finishing first.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide2.mp3` — Reading tab

> Anne hasn’t come down, and Marilla won’t say why. I’ve been hanging about the kitchen like a lost calf. This week’s watch is belonging, and I’ve been turning it over. I suppose a person can live in a house and not belong to it. As you read, watch the small things. Belonging doesn’t come with a speech. It shows up in some little thing a person does without thinking.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-wgrd.mp3` — Reading · What Good Readers Do

> At the end of a week, it’s worth looking back down the road you came. Think where Anne was when Marilla drove her off to White Sands, and where she’s got to now. Then find one small thing she does today that proves she’s moved. Look at whoever’s standing next to her, too. When one person changes, the folks around them mostly do as well.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-read.mp3` — Reading · Read: Ch. 10

> Just one stop today, at the very end, so read straight through till you get there. It’s a short chapter, but there’s a lot in it. When you reach the stop, don’t rush on to the questions. Sit with it a while and think about what one small thing might mean to somebody who’s never had it. I’d wager you’ll know the moment when you come to it.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-guide3.mp3` — Assignment tab

> Well, now you know. I went upstairs. I hadn’t been up there in four years, not since I helped Marilla paper the spare bedroom. I asked Anne if she’d just smooth it over with Rachel, and she said she’d do anything for me. Don’t tell Marilla I had a hand in it. She might think I was putting my oar in. Go on and say what you noticed, and show it from the book.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-chapter.mp3` — Assignment · Chapter Thinking

> The first question’s about me, and I’ll admit it’s a bit awkward. Why would a shy old fellow climb those stairs? That’s for you to work out. The second is about Anne saying sorry and Marilla not liking the way she did it, so think about what Anne was doing with her imagination. The last one has you look back over the whole week. Pick your moment, and say why.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-wordconn.mp3` — Assignment · Word Study Connection

> Your vocabulary answer is saved. One of this week’s words was penitent, which is somebody who’s truly sorry. Well, Anne said sorry to Rachel, and Rachel forgave her, and that’s what mattered to me. Whether she was penitent or just enjoying herself a good deal, I’ll leave to you. You’ve read it. I only heard about it.

## `matthew\g4ela-8-4-workshop.mp3` — Assignment · Writer’s Workshop

> Your job today is deciding how your character changes from start to finish. Take Anne. When this week began, nobody at Green Gables had asked for her. Now she’s settled, and I think she knows she’s wanted. That’s a long way to come in five chapters. Your character needn’t travel so far. They ought to end the story a little different from how they began, though.

---

**How he stays in character.** Shy, gentle, a man of few words, who shows how he feels by what he does.

**What he knows, and when.** Before the reading he doesn’t know what happened with Mrs. Rachel. Marilla only tells him at the start of Ch. 10. He just knows Anne hasn’t come down and Marilla won’t say why. Going upstairs, "smooth it over" and "don’t tell Marilla" come out only in the Assignment-tab clips. He wasn’t at Mrs. Rachel’s or on the walk home, so he never describes those scenes as if he saw them.

**Checked against the novel.** Everything he mentions is in Ch. 6–10: Marilla driving Anne to White Sands, not having been upstairs in his own house for four years (since he helped paper the spare bedroom), "just smooth it over", "I’d do anything for you", "don’t tell Marilla" and "putting my oar in", and Mrs. Rachel forgiving Anne. No clip reads the page, says a word part on its own, gives away an answer on the page, or names a time of day. Every clip is 40–75 words.
