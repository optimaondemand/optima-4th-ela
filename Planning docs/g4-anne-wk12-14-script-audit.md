## The structural note (rule 6)

The slot formulas repeat across all twelve lessons, so a student hears the same first five words from a different mouth every day:

- guide2 opens **"There's a box here about…"** in 11 of 12 (12.3 is "There is a box…").
- chapter opens **"The first question is/asks/wants…"** in 11 of 11.
- dol opens **"The top sentence is…"** in 10 of 11.
- read opens **"Two stops. The first comes when…"** or **"One stop, at the…"** in 9 of 11.
- guide3 opens **"Now you write" / "Now the writing" / "Now it's your turn"** in 10 of 12.
- guide1 opens **"Four bits/pieces of word work"** in 7 of 12.
- Day-1 morphology is "Two old roots today" ×3; day-2 vocabulary is "(The) same four words as yesterday" ×3.
- workshop opens "Today you add / cut / swap / score…" in 6 of 11. That's the page's "Today's focus" verb in the character's mouth, which is the lesson-plan-verb red flag in a milder form.

Shared lines across different characters: "your ear knows more than you think" (Diana 12.1 guide1, Gilbert 13.3 guide1); "That's where the thinking is" (Marilla 13.1 guide1, Gilbert 14.1 guide1); "Read it back/over once. You'll (always) find something" (Marilla 13.1 guide3, Gilbert 14.1 guide3); "When you write yours, choose your…" (all three RWM clips); "with something from the book" (four guide3s).

None of this is worth a re-record on its own. It's a template habit, and the fix is a rule for week 15 onward: the first sentence of every clip has to be one only that character would say, and the slot name ("box," "question," "stops") can't be the subject of it. If you ever re-record a whole lesson for other reasons, that's the moment to vary its openers.

## Who drifted toward a generic voice

1. **Gilbert (13.3, 14.1).** Easy and teasing in the welcomes and Watch clips, a study-skills tutor everywhere else. Before (14.1 guide3): "Now you write. Here's how I go about it at Queen's. Read the question twice. Decide exactly what it's asking. Answer that, and nothing else, then prove it with something from the book." After: "Now you write. Here's a trick from exam week. Read the question twice, and the second time look for the trap. Then find the line in the chapter before you write a word. Anne thinks that's cheating. It's checking."
2. **Diana (12.1, 13.2).** Lovely when she's talking about Anne, Ruby or herself; invisible in the word-work clips. Before (13.2 grammar): "Ask of each verb, could I watch somebody do it? If you couldn't, it might be one that only joins. Then try putting was in its place." After: "…the way I hide behind Anne when Josie Pye comes. I always find the first one and go skipping off, and then I have to come back. Ask of each verb, could I watch somebody do it? If not, try was in its place."
3. **Miss Stacy (12.4).** A teacher is allowed to teach, but half her clips are only that. Before (guide3): "Now it's your turn to write. For the first two questions, stay inside this chapter. Say what happened, and show me the line." After: "Now it's your turn, and I'll tell you what I tell my Queen's class: don't tell me, show me. For the first two, stay inside this chapter and put your finger on the line. Anne would pick five mistakes for the last one. Pick one."
4. **Marilla, but only in 13.1's word work.** Her reading and assignment clips are the strongest in the set; the morphology and copia clips could be anyone. Before (13.1 morphology): "They don't stand on their own; they live inside other words, the way a good habit lives inside a person." After: "They don't stand on their own. They sit inside other words the way the yeast sits inside the bread. You never see it, and you'd notice fast if it weren't there."
