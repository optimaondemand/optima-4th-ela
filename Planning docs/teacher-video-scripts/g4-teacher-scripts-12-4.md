# Lesson 12.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 30 · The Queen's Class Is Organized. Guide this lesson: Miss Stacy. Each script plays right after Miss Stacy's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-12-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week twelve, and it's a looking-back day. Over the week, Anne went from green hair to a boat sinking under her on the pond. In Chapter 30 things finally calm down, and she takes stock of where her life is headed. You'll do some looking back, too, summing up five chapters and reading your own story from start to finish. You already know how to do all of it.

## Welcome · g4ela-12-4-welcome

Earlier this week the chapters were full of scrapes. Chapter 30 is calmer, and a lot of it is about plans for the future. Quiet chapters still matter, though. Sometimes a person changes without anything dramatic happening at all, and they only notice later. As you read, keep two things in your head. Which parts of Anne seem the same as always, and which parts seem new?

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-12-4-dol

You've mended a run-on like this before. In lesson two you learned there are two ways to do it, and the model shows one of them with its comma and "but." Either way works on the bottom sentence, so choose the one that sounds best to you. One small extra about Latin. Languages always get a capital, but most school subjects, like math and science, stay lowercase.

## Before the word work · g4ela-12-4-guide1

You've already used two irregular verbs today, in the warm-up sentence about Anne learning Latin. Both tapping activities use rules from the start of the week. In grammar, think about why a writer might make a verb sound wrong. In spelling, think about what that quiet e does for a c or a g. If you can say the rule to yourself before you tap, you'll know what you're hunting for.

## Morphology · g4ela-12-4-morphology

You've lived with these two roots all week, in words like tractor and report. As you match, give the front parts some attention, too. Export carries things out of a country and import carries them in, so the front part changes which way the carrying goes. Also watch for look-alikes. A portion of pie has those same letters, but it isn't carrying anything.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-12-4-vocabulary

These four words have been with you since the first lesson, and two of them came right out of chapter titles. Vanity named the green hair chapter, and epoch named Anne's trip to town in Chapter 29. If you pick the option about your own character, think about which word really fits them. Is your character diligent, or do they give up? That one sentence might even give you a story idea.

## Grammar · g4ela-12-4-grammar

You already know why mistakes like these happen. Earlier this week we talked about little kids who learn the regular rule and then use it on every verb. That's the thinking to describe in your box. One spot to watch is a helper word like had in front of a verb. Some verbs change shape after a helper, the way she ate becomes she had eaten.

## Spelling · g4ela-12-4-spelling

Think back to the start of the week, when you sorted words by what happens to that e. The clue is always the letter right after the c or g, because some vowels keep those letters soft and some don't. If you get stuck, test the rule on a word that isn't in this draft, like peaceable, and see which way it goes. Then come back and look at the six.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-12-4-guide2

Earlier this week, we wondered whether Anne would actually act differently after a mistake. This box is where you start to find out. Read Anne's moment slowly, especially where she wishes she hadn't been so proud. Her anger at Gilbert goes all the way back to the slate she broke over his head in her first days at school. Some lessons take years to sink in, not days.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-12-4-wgrd

You already have a tool for this from the start of the week, the somebody, wanted, but, so, then frame. Today you stretch it across five chapters instead of one. Ask what Anne wanted over all of them, not in a single scrape. And keep checking for your opinion sneaking in. Two sentences joined with words like so and because will hold it all together.

## Before you read · g4ela-12-4-read

Chapter 30 covers months, not one afternoon, so watch for clues about time as you go. When the author skips ahead, that's the same leaving-out move you've been practicing for your summaries. Also think about what Miss Stacy's plan would mean for a girl Anne's age back then. Going on past the little Avonlea school took a lot of hard work and some very hard tests.

## Before you write · g4ela-12-4-guide3

Bring along the habits from earlier this week. Turn each question into the start of your first sentence, and use names instead of only "she." When you add a quote, set it up with a few words of your own, like "Anne admits," and copy only the part you need. The Growing-Up Watch box on the Reading tab is a good place to go back and look.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-12-4-chapter

You know to check every question for two parts, and each one here has them. For the second question, the hint underneath gives you a start, so spend most of your effort on the why. For the third, words like before and after can show how Anne changed. If you want a challenge, try the extra question about whether she could have learned it any other way.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-12-4-workshop

All week you've been revising, adding in one lesson and cutting in another. Today you see what that work did. When you name your strongest part, say why it works. Sam's Scene 3 is the fall he slowed down at the start of the week, with the grass and the gate. If a scene still feels thin, that's okay. Sam leaves his ending alone, because Week 14 is for endings.
