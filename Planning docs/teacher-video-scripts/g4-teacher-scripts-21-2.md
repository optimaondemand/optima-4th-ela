# Lesson 21.2 teacher video scripts

Day 2 · Ch. 6, Bound for the Promised Land. Freedom Train. Guide this lesson: Marcus (college history student, former walking-tour guide on the Eastern Shore). Each script plays right after Marcus's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 40 to 60 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment; it is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-21-2

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends, welcome back. Today's chapter is called Bound for the Promised Land, and everything changes for Harriet in it. First, stop at the Poetry Corner. The poem is Mother to Son, by Langston Hughes. Nothing in it rhymes, but read it out loud and you'll still hear a strong beat, because it sounds like a real mom talking. In grammar, you'll learn the -est ending, for comparing three or more things. And after you read the chapter, open the songs card. It shows what people nearby heard in Harriet's song, and what she really meant.


## Welcome · g4ela-21-2-welcome

A whole page in his notes tells you how big this chapter is. Here's something to help with the title. Remember Old Cudjoe reading the story of Moses under the oak tree? Moses led his people out of slavery in Egypt, toward a promised land. Watch for that same oak tree in this chapter, because Harriet goes back to it for a very important talk. When you finish, come back to the title and ask yourself: what is the promised land for Harriet?


## Freedom Watch · g4ela-21-2-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 1]

Which is scarier? I'm still not sure myself. Here's something to notice while you read. Harriet doesn't make her choice just once. First she makes it with her brothers. Then, after things go wrong, she has to make it all over again, by herself. Ask yourself which time was harder. The Think question on the page asks when you've picked the scarier path. In your journal, write one sentence about that time, and one sentence about who helped you, if anyone did.


## Before you read · g4ela-21-2-read

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

I love the way the Quakers talk. Try saying thee looks hot out loud. Here's something to watch for. When Harriet visits the Quaker lady, neither of them ever says the words run away. Harriet asks to buy eggs. The lady says, if I were traveling, here's the road I would take. They're talking in a kind of code, so read that part twice. One more thing. The lady says she can't sell eggs on First Day. That's what Quakers called Sunday.
