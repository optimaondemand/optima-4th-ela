# Lesson 20.3 teacher video scripts

Day 3 · Ch. 3, School Days. Freedom Train. Guide this lesson: Marcus (college history student, former walking-tour guide on the Eastern Shore). Each script plays right after Marcus's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 40 to 60 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment; it is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-20-3

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends. Today's chapter is called School Days, and here's the surprise: there is no school in it. Harriet never went to one. So today you're a detective, counting every person who teaches her something. You'll meet Old Cudjoe, who can read, and you'll hear songs that mean more than they seem to. After the reading, open the card called Songs With a Hidden Meaning. It shows what the overseer heard and what the singers meant. In Writer's Workshop, you'll choose one research question that's just the right size.


## Welcome · g4ela-20-3-welcome

A tally of teachers. Here's a way to set up your page. Draw a line down the middle. On the left, write each teacher's name when you meet them. On the right, write what they taught. Some teachers in this chapter are people, and one of them might not be a person at all. When you finish, look at the right side of your page. Those lessons together tell you what kind of school this really was.


## Freedom Watch · g4ela-20-3-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 1]

Learning what you're not supposed to know. The book tells us that across the South, teaching an enslaved person to read was against the law. Think about why someone would want a law like that. What were they afraid would happen if people could read? Keep that question in mind when Old Cudjoe reads the news aloud. Then, in your journal, write one thing you've learned this year outside of school, and who taught it to you.


## Before you read · g4ela-20-3-read

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

This chapter has a lot of songs in it, and it's easy to skim them. Please don't. Read each one slowly, maybe even out loud. Then ask yourself: is this song only about the Bible, or could it also be about the people singing it? Near the end, watch for one song the overseer likes because it sounds so peaceful. Notice what the workers do right after they sing it. That's your clue.
