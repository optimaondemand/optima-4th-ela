# Number the Stars final project · teacher video scripts

Page: lesson-19-nts-project.html (one day, after 19.4). Guide: Grete, the narrator. The lesson video plays at the top of the page. Each other script plays with Grete's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 40 seconds each; the lesson video runs about 2 minutes.

## Lesson video · g4ela-nts-project

Hi, friends. You finished Number the Stars. That's a whole book about a girl who was braver than she thought she could be, and today you get to show what it meant to you.

You'll pick one of three ways. You can fill a keepsake box for Ellen, light three windows on the night the war ends, or reveal the secrets hidden inside ordinary things. All three are graded the same way, so pick the one you can picture best.

Whichever way you choose, the steps walk you through four things, one screen at a time. First, three objects from the book: one from the beginning, one from the middle and one from the end. Second, a short note for each object, three or four sentences. Tell where it shows up, give a quote or an exact detail from your book, and tell why it matters. Third, one piece of real history from a history card, tied to one of your objects. And last, a closing paragraph about what this whole book says about courage.

Your project builds on the side of the page as you work, and your work saves as you go. When you're done, Step 7 turns it into a PDF. Put your name at the top first, then upload the PDF in Canvas. Take your time with the notes. That's where your thinking shows.


## Grete's welcome · g4ela-nts-project-welcome

A few things you could never throw away. I think everyone has a few of those. Before you choose, read all three ways, and open the model note under each one. Then ask yourself which one you can already picture. If you can see the box, or the street, or the secret, that's probably your way.


## Until Ellen Comes Home · g4ela-nts-project-ellen

A spare key in a sewing box. That's such a small way to say, we're waiting for you. In this way, every note is a letter. Start with Dear Ellen, and write to her like a best friend would, with I and you. And remember Grete's warning: Annemarie only knows what she saw or what someone told her. If she wasn't there, she can't tell it.


## Liberation Night · g4ela-nts-project-liberation

Light coming back on, one window at a time. I love picturing that street. In this way, each window has its own speaker, so each note should sound like that person. Kirsti won't sound like Uncle Henrik. Each time you pick an object, choose whose window it is, and if that person didn't live to see the end of the war, let someone who loved them do the talking.


## Peter's Hidden Messages · g4ela-nts-project-hidden

The safest secret looks dull. That's exactly why the soldiers missed so much. Every note in this way has two parts. On the outside tells what anyone would have seen. Really tells what it was doing. Before you write the Really part, check the chapter where the book explains it. Sometimes that's a few chapters later than you'd think.


## Model note: the necklace · g4ela-nts-project-model-ellen

Let's look at what that note does. It says when: the night the soldiers came. It gives an exact detail: the star pressed its shape into her hand. And it says why: so the soldiers would believe Ellen was her sister. It sounds like Annemarie, too. Where, a detail, why, and the right voice. Your notes need all four.


## Model note: Peter's seashell · g4ela-nts-project-model-liberation

Notice who's talking in that one. It's Annemarie, remembering Peter, because Peter didn't live to see this night. The note is short, but every sentence is doing a job. It names the object, says where it came from, and says why she keeps it in the window. Yours can be three or four sentences. Make every one count.


## Model note: the newspaper · g4ela-nts-project-model-hidden

Hear the two parts? On the outside, thin pages folded small. Really, a newspaper the Germans had banned. Then the note names the chapter and says why the secret mattered: someone could have been arrested. That last part is the one people forget. Don't just say what the secret was. Say why it had to stay hidden.
