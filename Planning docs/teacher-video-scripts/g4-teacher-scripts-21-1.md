# Lesson 21.1 teacher video scripts

Day 1 · Ch. 5, Not Worth a Sixpence. Freedom Train. Guide this lesson: Marcus (college history student, former walking-tour guide on the Eastern Shore). Each script plays right after Marcus's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 40 to 60 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment; it is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-21-1

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends, welcome to week twenty-one. Today's chapter is called Not Worth a Sixpence. A sixpence was a small coin that wasn't worth much at all, so you can guess that title isn't a kind one. This week, our reading job is chronology. That's a big word for the order things happen in. Sterling covers a lot of time in this chapter, so time words will be your best friends. On the Word Study tab, you'll start a brand-new quilt with two word parts: tele, which means far, and auto, which means self. And in Writer's Workshop, you begin gathering notes. Sam shows you how to keep each note short, with a page number on every one.


## Welcome · g4ela-21-1-welcome

Marcus said that title made him angry, and I think it's supposed to sting a little. But there's something tricky about it. The title is ironic. That means it says the opposite of what turns out to be true. Sterling wants you to spend the chapter proving those words wrong. As you read, keep two short lists. On one side, write what Master and the traders see when they look at Harriet. On the other side, write what they miss. I think your second list will be longer.


## Freedom Watch · g4ela-21-1-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 1]

I can see why Marcus keeps coming back to this part. Here's a phrase to watch for. Sterling says Harriet is still a slave in form, but in spirit she is a free woman. In form means on the outside, the way things look on paper. In spirit means on the inside, in her heart and her mind. Look at the Think question on the page. In your journal, write about a time someone guessed wrong about what you could do, and what you did next.


## Before you read · g4ela-21-1-read

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Marcus has walked those streets, so that part is worth slowing down for. A few things before you start. Early on, you'll learn that Harriet's injury left her with sudden sleeping spells, and they never went away. Later, Master lets Harriet hire her own time. That means she can work for other people, as long as she pays Master one dollar every week. Whatever is left over is hers. Some of what people say about her in this chapter is cruel. Keep reading. Harriet answers them with what she does.
