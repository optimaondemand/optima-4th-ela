# Lesson 13.3 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 33 · The Hotel Concert. Guide this lesson: Gilbert. Each script plays right after Gilbert's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-13-3

Hi, friends. It's Day 3 of week thirteen, so a lot of today will feel familiar. You already know linking verbs, comparing words, and the roots for looking and building, and today you build with them yourself. The new part is Chapter 33, where Anne recites at a grand hotel concert and freezes up for a moment. After that, you'll keep editing your story, this time hunting for wrong words, misspellings, and run-ons.

## Welcome · g4ela-13-3-welcome

Earlier this week, Anne noticed herself growing up, and then she saw her name tied at the top of the pass list. Today she faces something scarier than a test: a room full of strangers in fancy clothes. Stage fright happens to brave people too, even grown-ups who perform for a living. As you read, watch for what helps Anne find her voice again. Sometimes it's something very small.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-13-3-dol

You've been spotting linking verbs all week, so the top sentence should feel friendly. One thing is new here. A linking verb can join a person to a describing word, and it can also join them to a naming word that tells what someone is. Check which kind the model uses. For the bottom sentence, use the trick from Day 2: when a second person starts their own action or feeling, a break belongs there.

## Before the word work · g4ela-13-3-guide1

Think back to how this week's word work has gone. You noticed the patterns on Day 1 and found them in real sentences on Day 2. Today is the building day, the step that shows you truly own a skill. Make a real try before you open a hint, and read the explanation afterward, even on the ones you got right. That's where you find out whether you were right for the right reason.

## Morphology · g4ela-13-3-morphology

You met these two roots at the start of the week, and the examples box is there if you need a refresher. One new thing to notice today: a root can't stand on its own the way a base word like help can. It always needs a part attached on one side or both. That's why every line here is missing a piece. Use the meaning after each dash to check what the whole word should do.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-13-3-vocabulary

These four words should feel like old friends by now. You took them apart on Day 1 and met their word families on Day 2. Today they're back in sentences about people you know, like Anne, Jane, and Miss Stacy. Before you choose, picture each person in that exact moment and ask what they're like right then. When all three blanks are filled, try the extra word in each one as a final check.

## Grammar · g4ela-13-3-grammar

You learned the swap test earlier this week: put was in place of the verb and see if the sentence still makes sense. Every verb in today's bank passes that test, so the real question is which shade of meaning fits. Look at the hints if you need a nudge. Then for Show What You Know, choose a description that tells us something about what's going on inside Anne while she waits.

## Spelling · g4ela-13-3-spelling

On Days 1 and 2 you practiced each rule by itself, counting beats and changing a y to i. Today both have to work together in the same word. If you get stuck, warm up with a word that isn't on the list, like busy or tiny, and say its comparing form out loud first. Then come back and build yours. In Show What You Know, naming both rules is what proves you didn't just guess.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-13-3-guide2

On Day 2, the Growing-Up Watch showed Anne caring more about someone else's proud face than about a prize. Today's box takes that a step further, into choosing what really matters. Think back to the girl who once longed for puffed sleeves. As you read, hold her next to the Anne in this chapter. Then try the box's question about your own story character, and whether anyone else in your story would agree.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-13-3-wgrd

You've worked on theme every day this week. On Day 1 you learned a theme is a full sentence about life, and on Day 2 you learned the difference between stated and implied. Today's is implied. When you write your missing sentence, give it the same test as before: take Anne's name out, and it should still be true about real people. Then point to the moment in Chapter 33 that made you think it.

## Before you read · g4ela-13-3-read

On Day 2 you watched for the spot where the writing slows down to show a big moment. That move comes back today, so notice where Chapter 33 lingers. A little background helps, too. In Anne's time, concerts often meant people reciting poems from memory. Keep in mind that we only see Gilbert through Anne's eyes. At the first Pause and Think stop, ask how sure you can be about what his face means.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-13-3-rwm

You've seen this before. On Day 2, single quotation marks sat inside the regular ones, and they're back today around everything Anne says. Something new is the word Why at the start of her second sentence. She isn't asking anything. It's an old way of showing surprise, a bit like saying, Well, of course. Notice how she ends with more or less, too. That small, honest touch makes her sound like a real girl talking.

## Before you write · g4ela-13-3-guide3

Two of today's questions have two parts again, so use the trick from the start of the week and answer both sides of the and. Question three asks you to compare two girls, and joining words like but and while make that easy to follow. When you finish, give your answers the same check you're giving your story today. Look for their, there, and they're, and make sure each one is the right word.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-13-3-chapter

Comparing has been our big word all week, and every one of these questions is a comparison. In question one, check whether Anne's feelings about her own things stay the same from the dressing room to the end of the night. In question two, copy the words exactly as the book has them. In question three, notice that rich sits inside quotation marks. That's a clue Anne means it in a new way.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-13-3-workshop

This is your third editing pass this week. Capitals and end marks came first, then dialogue, and now it's word choice and run-ons. For the mix-ups in the chart, try stretching the word out. If they're or you're still makes sense as they are or you are, keep it. There has here hiding inside it, which helps with places. And look at Sam's before and after, because his run-on is the same kind you fixed in today's warm-up.
