# Lesson 13.3 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 33 · The Hotel Concert. Guide this lesson: Gilbert. Each script plays right after Gilbert's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-13-3

Hi, friends. It's Day 3 of week thirteen, so a lot of today will feel familiar. You already know linking verbs, comparing words, and the roots for looking and building, and today you build with them yourself. The new part is Chapter 33, where Anne recites at a grand hotel concert and freezes up for a moment. After that, you'll keep editing your story, this time hunting for wrong words, misspellings, and run-ons.

## Welcome · g4ela-13-3-welcome

Gilbert sounds awfully pleased to have been in that room, doesn't he? Earlier this week, Anne saw her name tied at the top of the pass list. Today she faces something scarier than a test: a room full of strangers in fancy clothes. Stage fright happens to brave people too, even grown-ups who perform for a living. As you read, watch for what helps Anne find her voice again. Sometimes it's something very small.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-13-3-dol

Anne really is fussy about her name. She wants it spelled with an e, every time. One thing is new in the model. A linking verb can join a person to a describing word, and it can also join them to a naming word that tells what someone is. Check which kind the model uses. For the bottom sentence, use the trick from Day 2: when a new person starts their own feeling, a break belongs there.

## Before the word work · g4ela-13-3-guide1

Gilbert at the blackboard is easy to picture, and I bet his ear really did save him. Think back to how this week's word work has gone. You noticed the patterns on Day 1 and found them in real sentences on Day 2. Today is the building day. Make a real try before you open a hint, and read the explanation afterward, even when you're right. That's how you know you were right for the right reason.

## Morphology · g4ela-13-3-morphology

Gilbert picked two great pictures there, and I'll let you decide which goes with which. You met these roots at the start of the week, and the examples box is there if you need a refresher. One new thing to notice today: a root can't stand alone the way a base word like help can. It always needs a part attached. Use the meaning after each dash to check what the whole word should do.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-13-3-vocabulary

Gilbert won't say which word fits him, and I have a guess of my own. These four words should feel like old friends by now. You took them apart on Day 1 and matched them on Day 2. Today they're back in sentences about people you know, like Anne, Jane, and Miss Stacy. Before you choose, picture each person in that exact moment and ask what they're like right then.

## Grammar · g4ela-13-3-grammar

Close cousins is a good way to put it. These verbs really do look alike. You learned the swap test earlier this week: put was in place of the verb and see if the sentence holds. Every word in today's bank passes that test, so the real question is which shade of meaning fits. Then for Show What You Know, choose a description that tells us what's going on inside Anne while she waits.

## Spelling · g4ela-13-3-spelling

I'd love to have watched one of those spelldowns. Those two never let each other win easily. On Days 1 and 2 you practiced each rule by itself. Today both have to work together in the same word. If you get stuck, warm up with a word that isn't on the list, like busy or tiny, and say its comparing form out loud first. In Show What You Know, naming both rules is what proves you didn't just guess.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-13-3-guide2

Gilbert's betting against the diamonds, and he sounds pretty sure of Anne. On Day 2, the Growing-Up Watch showed Anne caring more about Matthew's proud face than about beating anybody. Today's box takes that a step further, into choosing what really matters. Think back to the girl who once longed for puffed sleeves. As you read, hold her next to the Anne in this chapter and notice how much has changed.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-13-3-wgrd

Only smiling, Gilbert says, but in this chapter, a smile carries a lot. You've worked on theme all week. On Day 1 you learned a theme is a full sentence about life, and on Day 2 you sorted stated from implied. Today's is implied. When you write your missing sentence, give it the same test as before: take Anne's name out, and it should still be true about real people.

## Before you read · g4ela-13-3-read

Gilbert says he thinks she means it, and he sounds honest about that. On Day 2 you watched for the spot where the writing slows down to show a big moment. That move comes back today, so notice where Chapter 33 lingers. In Anne's time, concerts often meant people reciting poems from memory. Keep in mind that we see Gilbert through Anne's eyes, so ask how sure you can be about what his face means.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-13-3-rwm

Gilbert likes things in threes, and so do a lot of good speakers. You've seen this before. On Day 2, single quotation marks sat inside the regular ones, and they're back today around everything Anne says. Something new is the word Why at the start of her second sentence. She isn't asking anything. It's an old way of showing surprise, a bit like saying, Well, of course.

## Before you write · g4ela-13-3-guide3

Gilbert has a plan for everything, and that one's worth borrowing. Two of today's questions have two parts again, so use the trick from the start of the week and answer both sides of the and. Question three asks you to compare two girls, and joining words like but and while make that easy to follow. Before you submit, check that each answer says clearly which girl you mean, since Anne, Jane, and Diana all show up.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-13-3-chapter

Gilbert's a little too curious about question two, if you ask me. Comparing has been our big word all week, and two of these questions are comparisons too. In question one, check whether Anne's feelings about her own things stay the same from the dressing room to the end of the night. In question three, notice that rich sits inside quotation marks. That's a clue Anne means it in a new way.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-13-3-workshop

Checking beats guessing, Gilbert's right about that, and that's true for every writer. This is your third editing pass this week. Capitals and end marks came first, then dialogue, and now word choice and run-ons. For the mix-ups in the chart, try stretching the word out. If they're or you're still makes sense as they are or you are, keep it. There has here hiding inside it, which helps with places. And Sam's run-on is the same kind you fixed in today's warm-up.
