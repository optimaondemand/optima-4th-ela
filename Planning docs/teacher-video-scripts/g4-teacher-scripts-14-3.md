## Lesson video · g4ela-14-3

Hi, friends. Today is Chapter 37, and the title tells you this one is sad. Matthew dies in this chapter. I wanted you to know before you start, so it doesn't catch you by surprise. ## Welcome · g4ela-14-3-welcome

Marilla asked you to go gently, and I'm asking the same. Be kind to yourself today. You've known Matthew since Chapter 2, when he drove to Bright River and found Anne waiting. He was the first one at Green Gables who wanted her to stay. Later, he noticed she longed for puffed sleeves and gave her that brown dress at Christmas. Keep those moments close as you read. Feeling sad for Matthew is one way of remembering him.

## Before the word work · g4ela-14-3-guide1

I'm grateful Marilla shared that. Steady, ordinary work can help on a heavy day. You already know these roots. Script and scribe mean to write, and ject means to throw. Read the clue beside each blank and ask which action it describes. You also know helping verbs set the time. In your warm-up, would points ahead to something that hadn't happened yet. Your spelling uses the same three rules you've practiced all week.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-14-3-guide2

That's honest of Marilla, admitting she didn't see it at first. The Growing-Up Watch box has a line worth reading twice. Being grown up is not feeling less. Some readers think a character who doesn't cry must not care. Anne shows that isn't true. All week you've put the old Anne next to the new one. Today, picture the younger Anne in this same hard moment. Read slowly, and stop at both Pause and Think questions.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-14-3-rwm

Marilla doesn't often talk about her soft side, so it's tender that she points to this sentence herself. Wept her heart out is an old way of saying someone cried as hard as she could. Look at wept and crept. Neither one ends in e-d, so this week's suffix rules don't fit them. Sleep and slept work the same way. Notice how short both sentences are, too. When something enormous happens, Montgomery doesn't pile up words.

## Before you write · g4ela-14-3-guide3

I'm glad Marilla says the laughing surprised her too. Grief can surprise grown-ups as well. Each question has two parts, so check that your answer covers both. The hint under each one shows you where to look. You've answered questions like these all week, so trust what you know. Then you'll write a dedication like Sam's, for his mom, who told him to try anyway. A dedication can go to someone beside you, or to someone you miss.
