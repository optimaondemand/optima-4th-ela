# Lesson 14.3 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 37 · The Reaper Whose Name Is Death. Guide this lesson: Marilla. Each script plays right after Marilla's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each.

## Lesson video · g4ela-14-3

Hi, friends. Today is Chapter 37, and the title tells you this one is sad. Matthew dies in this chapter. I wanted you to know before you start, so it doesn't catch you by surprise. The page is quieter today. You'll warm up, do your word work, and then read. After that, you'll answer three questions and make a title page for your story. If you feel sad while you read, that's okay. Take a break whenever you need one.

## Welcome · g4ela-14-3-welcome

You've known Matthew since Chapter 2, when he drove to Bright River and found Anne waiting for him. He was the first person at Green Gables who wanted her to stay. Later, he noticed she longed for puffed sleeves and gave her that brown dress at Christmas. He showed his love in small, quiet ways. Keep those moments close today. Feeling sad for Matthew is one way of remembering him.

## Before the word work · g4ela-14-3-guide1

You already know these roots from the start of the week. Script and scribe mean to write, and ject means to throw. Read the little clue beside each blank and ask which action it describes. In grammar, you know helping verbs set the time. Look back at your warm-up sentence, where Anne hoped the tears would come. That word would points ahead to something that hadn't happened yet. Your spelling uses the same three rules you've practiced all week.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-14-3-guide2

The Growing-Up Watch box has a line worth reading twice. Being grown up is not feeling less. Some readers think a character who doesn't cry must not care very much. Anne shows that isn't true. All week you've put the old Anne next to the new one. Today the What Good Readers Do box asks you to picture the younger Anne in this same hard moment. Read slowly, and stop at both Pause and Think questions.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-14-3-rwm

Wept her heart out is an old way of saying someone cried as hard as she could. Look at wept and crept. Neither one ends in e-d, so the suffix rules you've practiced this week don't fit them. Sleep and slept work the same way. Notice how short both sentences are, too. When something enormous happens, Montgomery doesn't pile up words. Try keeping your Mimic sentences short like hers.

## Before you write · g4ela-14-3-guide3

Each question has two parts, so check that your answer covers both. The hint under each one shows you where in the chapter to look. You've answered questions like these all week, so trust what you know. Then, in the workshop, you'll write a dedication like Sam's, for his mom, who told him to try anyway. A dedication can go to someone right beside you, or to someone you miss. Either one is a good choice.
