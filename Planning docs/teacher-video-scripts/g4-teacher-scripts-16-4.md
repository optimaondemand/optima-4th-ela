## Welcome · g4ela-16-4-welcome

Paper dolls and grumpy shoes. I have a feeling Kirsti and I would have argued about shoes too. Notice how fast this chapter changes mood. Keep track of the exact moment it stops feeling like a regular evening, and mark it in your book or journal.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-16-4-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

Adding up the clues is a great way to finish the week. Make a quick list in your journal of every sign you've noticed since Chapter 1. Then read it from top to bottom. You might be surprised by what the list says all together. One sign is a hint. Four signs are a pattern.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-16-4-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: interrupt · conduct · relocate · suspicion]

No misses at all? I believe in you. And the one-sentence challenge is tricky. If you can't fit all four, try two in one sentence and two in another. That still counts as a win. Read your sentence out loud when you're done, and listen for whether it sounds like the book.


## Grammar · g4ela-16-4-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

Owner-words never take an apostrophe, but names do. Hold on to that one. For every apostrophe in the draft, try reading the word as two words, like it is or they are. If that makes sense, the apostrophe can stay. If it doesn't, ask whether the word is a name. A name can keep it. An owner-word can't.


## Spelling · g4ela-16-4-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Last check of the week for our hiding vowel. Some of these are already right, so go slow. Say the related word, then use your spelling voice if you need it. If both agree with the letters on the screen, you can trust that word and leave it alone.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-16-4-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 4]

Frightened and proud at the same time. That's a big feeling for a ten-year-old. Being trusted with something important is a kind of courage too. Think about a time a grown-up trusted you with something. How did it feel? Keep that feeling in mind as you read the end of the chapter.


## Before you read · g4ela-16-4-read

[ON SCREEN: Stop 3]

Asking why the author put the funny part first is a great question. Think about how a scary scene feels when it comes right after a laugh. As you read, notice what the grown-ups say, and also what they don't say. The silences in this chapter matter as much as the words.


## Before you write · g4ela-16-4-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q3]

What Annemarie learns, one careful word and a changed life. Grete gave you the map. When a question asks how her life is different, compare. Say what it was like at the start of the chapter, then what's true by the end. Use a detail for each half.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-16-4-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q2]

The difference between the polite word and the truth. That's a sharp way to put it. Look at how carefully Papa speaks. When grown-ups choose their words that slowly, it's usually because the truth is hard to say. Use the chapter to explain what Annemarie figures out, and how you can tell.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-16-4-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

Proof that anyone could find. That's the key. Write the chapter number next to each piece of evidence. That way, if someone doubts your reason, you can send them straight to the page. If a reason has no proof yet, it might be time to swap it for a stronger one.
