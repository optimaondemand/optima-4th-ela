# Lesson 17.3 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 3 · Ch. 7, The House by the Sea. Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 40 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment: click a button in it and that card goes up on your video. It is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-17-3

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends. Today we leave the city behind. This painting shows an old farmhouse on the same stretch of coast where Uncle Henrik lives. On the Read tab, the Museum Case holds a real passport stamped in Sweden. The Then and Now pictures let you stand at Gilleleje and look out to sea, once in a painting from 1930 and once in a photograph. Look closely at the far edge of the water. Writer's Workshop is where your first body paragraph happens today.


## Welcome · g4ela-17-3-welcome

Smelling the sea after a chapter full of soldiers. I can almost feel the breeze. Keep Grete's question about small objects in your pocket while you read. One object matters a lot in this chapter, even though nobody holds it. Watch how few words Annemarie uses about it.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-17-3-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

A quiet promise. Those can be the hardest kind of courage to spot. Try drawing a line down a page in your journal. Feelings go on the left, actions on the right. When you finish, circle the one action you would use as proof. If your circle lands on something small and quiet, that's fine. Quiet actions count.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-17-3-dol

[ON SCREEN: Sentence B · Show the fix]

Two girls and one country, all needing capitals. I'll start with a different sentence about the girls at the sea. Ask, is this the name of one special person or place? If it is, it stands tall. The sea and the meadow aren't names, so they stay small. Then find where one thought ends. Now try yours.


## Before the word work · g4ela-17-3-guide1

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule · Spelling rule]

Study the model first. That's like watching someone tie a knot before you try it yourself. Read the model out loud, one word at a time, and notice exactly what changed between before and after. On that last line, decide what you think before you look at anything else. Trust the work you did in the first two lessons.


## Morphology · g4ela-17-3-morphology

[ON SCREEN: Word parts]

Halfway across the water already. I love watching that beam grow. Think back to Chapter 5, when Ellen had to act like someone she wasn't, with an officer staring right at her. Keep that moment in mind when the word about acting as if something is true comes along.


## Vocabulary · g4ela-17-3-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: discreet]

If you can explain a word to someone else, it belongs to you. So try it with discreet. Mama gives the girls a warning in this chapter about other people. Is she asking them to be discreet? Explain why in one sentence. Then see if tense fits anyone at the house by the sea, too.


## Grammar · g4ela-17-3-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

Whispering and trusting your ear is a great trick. Try one more. If you're stuck between two verbs, swap the pronoun for a name. She walks becomes Ellen walks. They walk becomes the girls walk. A name often makes the right choice obvious. Then switch the pronoun back and check it once more.


## Spelling · g4ela-17-3-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Hooking cars onto a train. What a perfect way to picture it. The base word is the engine. Look at the model, though. Where two pieces hook together, a letter can double right at the joint. So spell the base first, hook on one piece at a time, and check every joint before you add the next car.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-17-3-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 3]

A box Grete never opened and never told about. That's a promise kept. Annemarie makes a promise like that, too. Notice that she doesn't explain much to Ellen. Sometimes keeping something safe means saying very little. As you read, ask yourself whether keeping a promise without any fuss takes courage.


## Before you read · g4ela-17-3-read

[ON SCREEN: Stop 2]

A meadow, a kitten and the sea. Enjoy every bit of it. But look for the moment Mama brings the danger right back. And when Annemarie points across the water, open the Then and Now on the Read tab and drag the line. The low dark line on the horizon in the photograph is Sweden. That's how close it is.


## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-17-3-rwm

Three jobs in one sentence. Let's watch it do all three.

[QUOTE ON SCREEN: press 6, read the line, press 6 again]

This one is from Chapter 7: "‘I took Ellen down to show her the sea,’ Annemarie explained when they reached the place where Mama waited."

Notice the verb explained. It's stronger than said, because it tells us Annemarie is giving a reason. For your Mimic, write something you once said, then a verb like explained, then when.


## Before you write · g4ela-17-3-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q1]

The necklace, Ellen, and Sweden. Grete just handed you the three big ideas. For the first question, the book asks you to find a sentence. Copy Annemarie's words exactly, inside quotation marks, and plug them into a sentence of your own. Then add one more sentence that says what her words show about her.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-17-3-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q3]

A free country so close you can almost see it. That's worth thinking about. Look at what Ellen says when she sees the far shore. Why would that thought matter so much to a Jewish girl in Denmark in 1943? Use what you learned about Sweden earlier in this unit, but only what you know so far.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-17-3-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

A sandwich with a purpose. I'm going to be hungry now. Check yours. Does your first sentence name a reason? Is your quote copied exactly from the book? Does your last sentence use a word from your claim? Three yeses mean it's ready. If you get a no, fix just that one part.
