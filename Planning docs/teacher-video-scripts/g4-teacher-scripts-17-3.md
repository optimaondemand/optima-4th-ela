# Lesson 17.3 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 3 · Ch. 7, The House by the Sea. Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 40 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment: click a button in it and that card goes up on your video. It is never read aloud.

## Welcome · g4ela-17-3-welcome

A small object carrying a lot of weight. Keep that question in your pocket. Here's a hint. Watch for an object that isn't even in the room. Ellen asks about it at bedtime, and Annemarie's answer is very short. Short answers can still say a lot.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-17-3-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

Two lists, feeling and doing. Try drawing a line down the middle of a page in your journal. Feelings go on the left, actions on the right. When you finish the chapter, circle the one action you'd use to prove Annemarie is brave.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-17-3-dol

[ON SCREEN: Sentence A · Show the fix]

Here's a quick capital check. Ask, is this the name of one special person or place? Ellen and Annemarie are names, so they stand tall. So is Sweden. The meadow and the water aren't names, so they stay lowercase.


## Before the word work · g4ela-17-3-guide1

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule · Spelling rule]

Studying the model first is like watching someone tie a knot before you try it. Look at the model slowly, then try yours. And on that last line, cover the answer choices and decide what you think before you look.


## Morphology · g4ela-17-3-morphology

[ON SCREEN: Word parts]

The word about acting as if something is true is pretend. Think back to Chapter 5. Ellen had to pretend to be Lise, and she did it with a soldier staring right at her. Pretending stretches the truth, which is why the stretching root is inside it.


## Vocabulary · g4ela-17-3-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: discreet]

Here's a word you can feel on the page today. When Mama tells the girls to stay away from people, she's asking them to be discreet. See if you can find another discreet moment before the chapter ends.


## Grammar · g4ela-17-3-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

Whispering is a great trick. Here's one more. If you're stuck between two verbs, swap the pronoun for a name. She walks becomes Ellen walks. They walk becomes the girls walk. Hearing it with a name often makes the right choice obvious.


## Spelling · g4ela-17-3-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Train cars, I love that. Here's the rule to remember. The base word keeps its spelling. If the base has a tricky middle, it stays tricky when you hook on more cars. Spell the base first, then add each piece.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-17-3-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 3]

Grete never opening that box is a promise kept. Annemarie makes a promise like that, too. Notice that she doesn't explain everything to Ellen. Sometimes keeping something safe means saying very little.


## Before you read · g4ela-17-3-read

[ON SCREEN: Stop 2]

The peaceful part is real, so enjoy it. But look for one sentence where Mama brings the danger right back. And when Annemarie points across the water, slide the Then and Now on the Read tab. The low dark line on the horizon is Sweden.


## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-17-3-rwm

Grete called it a tail. Let's look at the whole sentence.

[QUOTE ON SCREEN: press 6, read the line, press 6 again]

Here's the sentence from Chapter 7: "‘I took Ellen down to show her the sea,’ Annemarie explained when they reached the place where Mama waited."

Notice the verb explained. It's stronger than said, because it tells us Annemarie is giving a reason. For your Mimic, write something you said once, then use a verb like explained or promised, then add when.


## Before you write · g4ela-17-3-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q1]

For the first question, the book asks you to find a sentence. Look near the end of the chapter, at bedtime. Copy her words exactly, inside quotation marks, and plug them into a sentence of your own, the way you practiced.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-17-3-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q3]

For the Sweden question, use what the girls actually see and say. Ellen looks across and says it's not so very far. Why would that thought matter so much to a Jewish girl in Denmark in 1943?


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-17-3-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

Here's a checklist for your sandwich. Does your first sentence name a reason? Does your quote come from the book, copied exactly? Does your last sentence use a word from your claim? If you can say yes three times, your paragraph is ready.
