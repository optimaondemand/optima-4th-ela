# Lesson 20.2 teacher video scripts

Day 2 · Ch. 2, Peck of Trouble. Freedom Train. Guide this lesson: Marcus (college history student, former walking-tour guide on the Eastern Shore). Each script plays right after Marcus's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 40 to 60 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment; it is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-20-2

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends, welcome back. Today's chapter is called Peck of Trouble, and it begins inside the cabin where Harriet's family lives. Look closely at that cabin when you read. Sterling describes it so carefully it's almost like a room in a museum. Today is also the first day of our new poem, Sympathy, by Paul Laurence Dunbar. Marcus reads the whole poem to you, and you'll see four lines in gold. Those are the lines we'll learn by heart over the next few weeks. In Writer's Workshop, you'll brainstorm questions for your own research.


## Welcome · g4ela-20-2-welcome

Won't give in, even in small ways. Think about what a small way looks like. Not running away, not shouting, just quietly refusing to do one thing. As you read, notice the moment Harriet's mother asks her for something, and how Harriet answers. Then notice her father's answer, because it might surprise you. He doesn't agree with her mother. When two people who love Harriet disagree about what's safest for her, that's worth stopping to think about.


## Freedom Watch · g4ela-20-2-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 1]

This is a hard chapter, and Marcus was right to say take it slowly. Harriet is punished, and Sterling does not hide that. If it feels heavy, that's because it was heavy. Before you read on, think about this. Harriet says something inside her won't let her smile at someone who treats her cruelly. Is that stubbornness, or is it strength? Maybe it's both. Write one sentence in your journal with your answer, and one reason from the chapter.


## Before you read · g4ela-20-2-read

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Keep your four columns going: who, what happened, where, and why it matters. In this chapter, the where is mostly one small room, the cabin, so your where column might look the same all the way down. That's fine. Pay extra attention to the quilt. Notice how Old Rit made it and what the family thinks of it. Then watch for when it shows up again near the end of the chapter. When a writer brings an object back, it's for a reason.
