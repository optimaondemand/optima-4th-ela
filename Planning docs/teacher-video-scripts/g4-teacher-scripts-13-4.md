# Lesson 13.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 34 · A Queen's Girl. Guide this lesson: Anne. Each script plays right after Anne's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-13-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week thirteen. All week you've watched Anne grow, from her summer outdoors in Chapter 31 to the pass list in Chapter 32. Today, in Chapter 34, she packs up for Queen's. You already know this week's linking verbs and comparing words, so today you hunt for mistakes in them. Then you'll test a theme against a second chapter and read your story aloud for its last edit before you publish next week.


## Welcome · g4ela-13-4-welcome

Anne worried about giving a speech, and honestly, I'd have listened to one. It's fine that she's still deciding. Think back to her very first days at Green Gables, when she begged Marilla to call her Cordelia. That girl wished she could be somebody else. Now she's leaving home and working hard to stay herself. As you read, look for the moment where those two Annes feel furthest apart.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-13-4-dol

I'm with Anne on lonesomer. It sounds exactly like the feeling it describes. You've found linking verbs in the model all week, so that part should feel familiar. Look at the small word herself, too. It points back to Anne, so you know who's alone in that hall bedroom. When you fix the second sentence, read it aloud and listen for where your voice wants to rest.


## Before the word work · g4ela-13-4-guide1

Poor Anne and her geometry. I'm glad she can laugh about it now. Catching mistakes is the last step in the order you've followed since the start of the week. As you work through today's review, notice which slips are hardest for you to spot. Maybe it's the verbs, or maybe it's the endings. Jot your trouble spots in the back of your notebook so you know what to slow down on next time.


## Morphology · g4ela-13-4-morphology

Anne adores roots, and so do I. They make long words feel friendly. Spectators and structures came up at the start of the week, both built from these same two roots. Today's matching adds two front parts. Use the examples box on this page as your checker. After you make a match, plug it into one of those example words and see whether the meaning still holds up.


## Vocabulary · g4ela-13-4-vocabulary

I'd happily try every choice on that menu too, Anne, so we're alike there. You've lived with these four words since the start of the week. You already took broadminded and tactful apart into smaller words, so if the word-parts choice appeals to you, half the work is done. Whichever you pick, tie it to Chapter 34. Anne's ambitions get quite a push before this chapter ends.


## Grammar · g4ela-13-4-grammar

I believe Anne about her busy verbs. She was never one to sit still. At the start of the week, you saw that sense verbs like smelled can switch jobs, and later you watched felt do the same. A verb that can do both jobs is the easiest one to mislabel, so don't decide from the word alone. Look at what comes right after the verb on each card before you tap.


## Spelling · g4ela-13-4-spelling

Anne used to love a grand word, and I'll miss that a little. All week you've sorted comparing words by how many syllables they have, and that count is still your best tool. Before you tap, clap out the base word with its ending taken off. Then ask which rule in the notes box fits a word that size. Save the rule's name for your Show What You Know box.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-13-4-guide2

Nobody hands you that answer all at once, and Anne figured that out bit by bit. Chapter 31 had a nature picture in its title, a brook widening into a river. Anne's line in the Growing-Up Watch box uses one too, a tree that's been pruned. Gardeners snip off branches on purpose so a tree grows stronger and keeps its shape. Keep that picture in mind while you read.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-13-4-wgrd

Trying a theme on in two mirrors is such an Anne way to say it, and it works. A theme, as you learned at the start of the week, is a whole sentence the story believes about life. Today you're testing it, so watch its size. If it fits every book ever written, it's too big to test. Adding when or because usually shrinks it to something you can check against Chapters 31 through 34.


## Before you read · g4ela-13-4-read

Anne promising not to be dramatic made me smile. That might be the biggest change in the whole book. Queen's is in Charlottetown, too far from Avonlea to travel back and forth each day, so Anne has to live in town. Think back to Josie Pye, the girl who dared Anne to walk the ridgepole of a roof. Notice how Anne feels when Josie turns up in that lonely week.


## Before you write · g4ela-13-4-guide3

I'd have loved reading Anne's long answers with a poem on the end, but Miss Stacy had a point. Question three looks across four chapters, so you'll need to find your way around the book. The chapter titles help. Today's is "A Queen's Girl," and the first lesson's was "Where the Brook and River Meet." Skim the beginnings and endings, since big moments often land there.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-13-4-chapter

I agree that the third question is the hardest, and I'd also call it the most fun. The challenge on question one asks whether Anne is right that she hasn't really changed. You've been collecting changes all week, starting with the two inches she grew. A strong answer can agree with part of what she says and question another part, as long as you explain why.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-13-4-workshop

It really isn't bragging, Anne. Writers need to know their own strengths. Earlier this week you caught run-ons by listening for where your voice dropped. Sam's before and after shows that same move working on a whole scene. Slow down on the checklist line about saying a word that isn't on the page. Sometimes the word your mouth chose is better than the one you wrote.
