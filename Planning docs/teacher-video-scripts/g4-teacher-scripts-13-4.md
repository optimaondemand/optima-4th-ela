# Lesson 13.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 34 · A Queen's Girl. Guide this lesson: Anne. Each script plays right after Anne's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-13-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week thirteen. All week you've watched Anne grow, from her summer outdoors in Chapter 31 to the pass list in Chapter 32. Today, in Chapter 34, she packs up for Queen's. You already know this week's linking verbs and comparing words, so today you hunt for mistakes in them. Then you'll test a theme against a second chapter and read your story aloud for its last edit before you publish next week.

## Welcome · g4ela-13-4-welcome

Think back to Anne's very first days at Green Gables, when she begged Marilla to call her Cordelia. That little girl wished she could be somebody else. Today she's sixteen and leaving home, and she's working hard to stay exactly herself. That's a big turn for one character to make. As you read, look for the moment where the old Anne and this one feel furthest apart.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-13-4-dol

You've spotted linking verbs in the model every day this week, so this one should feel familiar. Look at the small word herself near the middle. It points straight back to Anne, the subject. When you fix the second one, read it aloud and listen for where your voice wants to rest. Then check your own story for that same kind of jam-up.

## Before the word work · g4ela-13-4-guide1

Catching mistakes is the last step in the order you've followed since Day 1. As you work through today's review, notice which slips are hardest for you to spot. Maybe it's the verbs, or maybe it's the endings. Jot your trouble spots in the back of your notebook. Next week you publish your story, and a short list of your own usual slips makes that final check a lot faster.

## Morphology · g4ela-13-4-morphology

Spectators and structures came up at the start of the week, both built from these same two roots. Today's matching adds two front parts. Use the examples box on this page as your checker. After you make a match, plug it into one of those example words and see whether the meaning still holds up. If it doesn't, try the other card before you move on.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-13-4-vocabulary

You've lived with these four words since the start of the week. You already took broadminded and tactful apart into smaller words, and you heard where ambition came from. So if the word-parts choice on the menu appeals to you, half the work is done. Whichever you pick, try to tie it to Chapter 34. Anne's ambitions get quite a push before this chapter ends.

## Grammar · g4ela-13-4-grammar

At the start of the week, you saw that sense verbs like smelled can switch jobs, and in the next lesson you watched felt do the same thing. Keep that in mind here, because a verb that can do both jobs is the easiest one to mislabel. Don't decide from the word alone. In each sentence on this card, look at what comes right after the verb before you tap.

## Spelling · g4ela-13-4-spelling

All week you've sorted comparing words by how many syllables they have. That count is still your best tool today. Before you tap anything, clap out the base word with its ending taken off. Then ask which rule in the notes box fits a word that size, and whether this word followed it. Save the rule's name, too, for your Show What You Know box.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-13-4-guide2

Chapter 31 had a nature picture right in its title, a brook widening into a river. Anne's line in the Growing-Up Watch box uses a nature picture too, a tree that's been pruned. Gardeners snip off branches on purpose so a tree grows stronger and keeps its shape. Hold on to that idea when you answer the question at the bottom of the box about your own character.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-13-4-wgrd

A theme, as you learned at the start of the week, is a whole sentence the story believes about life, and in the next lesson you learned it can be stated or implied. Today's step is testing it. Watch the size of your sentence. If it fits every book ever written, it's too big to test. Adding when or because usually shrinks it to something you can check against Chapters 31 through 34.

## Before you read · g4ela-13-4-read

Queen's is in Charlottetown, too far from Avonlea to travel back and forth each day, so Anne has to live in town. Think back to Josie Pye, the girl who dared Anne to walk the ridgepole of a roof. Notice how Anne feels when Josie turns up. The Pause and Think question asks about one line Anne says to Marilla, so read that scene slowly.

## Before you write · g4ela-13-4-guide3

Question three looks across four chapters, so you'll need to find your way around the book. The chapter titles help. Today's is "A Queen's Girl," and the first lesson's was "Where the Brook and River Meet." Skim the beginnings and endings, since big moments often land there. When your answers are done, read them aloud, the same way you'll read your story in Writer's Workshop.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-13-4-chapter

The challenge on question one asks whether Anne is right that she hasn't really changed. You've been collecting changes all week, starting with the two inches she grew. A strong answer can agree with part of what she says and question another part, as long as you explain why. For question two, compare the new dream with what Anne was hoping for when the week began.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-13-4-workshop

Earlier this week you caught run-ons by listening for where your voice dropped. Sam's before and after shows that same move working on a whole scene. Slow down on the checklist line about saying a word that isn't on the page. Sometimes the word your mouth chose is better than the one you wrote. And when you name what you're proud of, point to one exact moment.
