# Lesson 16.3 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 3 · Ch. 3, Where Is Mrs. Hirsch? Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each.

## Lesson video · g4ela-16-3

Hi, friends. You've met the soldiers on the corner and heard the story of the king. Today, the girls walk to a little shop they've known forever. Before you read, open the Museum Case on the Read tab to see what a wartime kitchen looked like. After the chapter, you'll study one more sentence the way a writer would. In Writer's Workshop, your claim gets three reasons.


## Welcome · g4ela-16-3-welcome

A broken button doesn't sound like the start of anything big. That's what makes it work. Big changes in this book keep showing up in small, ordinary errands. Watch how a trip to a shop turns into a question nobody wants to answer.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-16-3-wgrd

Who did this, and who is it aimed at? Those two questions work almost anywhere. Last time you looked for the gap between then and now. Today the clue sits right on a door. Don't just notice the object. Notice how the girls react to it, because their faces tell you how serious it is.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-16-3-dol

Grete covered the run-together part, so here's one extra. Mrs. ends with a period, even in the middle of a sentence. That's because it's a shortened word. The same goes for Mr. and Dr. The period there doesn't end the sentence. It just shows something was left out.


## Before the word work · g4ela-16-3-guide1

Building is the hard step, and you're ready for it. Sorting and hunting got you here. When you type an answer, say it in the sentence first, then type. And remember the word bank is a help, not a list you have to use in order.


## Morphology · g4ela-16-3-morphology

Crossing the sky to a smaller group of stars. I'll be cheering for you. Grete's tip about the front part is a good one. When a word has a beginning you didn't expect, it changes the whole meaning. So read the full meaning before you pick a root.


## Vocabulary · g4ela-16-3-vocabulary

Explaining a word to someone else really is the best test. If there's a grown-up nearby, try explaining suspicion to them right now. If you can do it without looking at the card, it's yours.


## Grammar · g4ela-16-3-grammar

That apostrophe trap catches grown-ups all the time, so don't feel bad if it tricks you once. Before you type, ask the whose question from earlier this week. Then check the end of the sentence. If no noun comes after the blank, you need the stand-alone kind.


## Spelling · g4ela-16-3-spelling

Saying the related word is the trick to use here. Try your spelling voice too, the one where every vowel is loud. Between the two, that hiding letter doesn't stand a chance.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-16-3-guide2

Not walking past. That's a small choice, and it matters. It can feel easier not to ask about something sad or scary. But asking a grown-up you trust is often the brave thing. Think of a time you asked a hard question, even when part of you didn't want to know the answer.


## Before you read · g4ela-16-3-read

Watching the door closely and noticing the time of the visit. Grete's giving you a lot to watch. Annemarie's Path shows where the shop is, right near the girls' building. As you read, think about what it means that this is happening on her own corner, not somewhere far away.


## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-16-3-rwm

The word something is doing a lot of quiet work in this one.

[QUOTE ON SCREEN: press 6, read the line, press 6 again]

Here's the sentence from Chapter 3: "There was something frightening about his being here at night."

Notice the sentence ends with at night. The writer saves the time for last, so it lands. For your Mimic, end your sentence with the detail that makes it feel strange.


## Before you write · g4ela-16-3-guide3

Grete laid out the questions, so here's one reminder. When you describe the door, use the details from the chapter, the way a camera would see them. Save your feelings about it for after the description.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-16-3-chapter

Thinking about Peter's visit as a clue is just right. A clue is something small that points to something bigger. So answer the first part with facts, then add a sentence about what the clue might be pointing to.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-16-3-workshop

Three reasons about three different things. That's what Sam does well. Check that yours don't all say the same idea in different words. If two reasons sound like twins, swap one for something new.
