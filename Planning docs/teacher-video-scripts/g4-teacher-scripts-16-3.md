# Lesson 16.3 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 3 · Ch. 3, Where Is Mrs. Hirsch? Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 40 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment: click a button in it and that card goes up on your video. It is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-16-3

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends. You've met the soldiers on the corner and heard the story of the king. Today, the girls walk to a little shop they've known forever. Before you read, open the Museum Case on the Read tab to see what a wartime kitchen looked like. After the chapter, you'll study one more sentence the way a writer would. In Writer's Workshop, your claim gets three reasons.


## Welcome · g4ela-16-3-welcome

A broken button doesn't sound like the start of anything big. That's what makes it work. Big changes in this book keep showing up in small, ordinary errands. Watch how a trip to a shop turns into a question nobody wants to answer, and notice who finally asks it.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-16-3-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

Who did this, and who is it aimed at? Those two questions work almost anywhere. Last time you looked for the gap between then and now. Today the clue sits right on a door. Don't just notice the object. Notice how the girls react to it, because their faces tell you how serious it is.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-16-3-dol

[ON SCREEN: Sentence B · Show the fix]

Two thoughts run together again. You're getting good at spotting those. Watch this other sentence first. Notice that Mrs. ends with a period, even in the middle of a sentence, because it's a shortened word. The same goes for Mr. and Dr. That period doesn't end anything. Now find where your first thought really ends.


## Before the word work · g4ela-16-3-guide1

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule · Spelling rule]

Building is the hard step, and you're ready for it. Sorting and hunting got you here. When you type an answer, say the whole sentence out loud first, then type. And remember the word bank is a help, not a list you have to use in order. Each word in it gets used once.


## Morphology · g4ela-16-3-morphology

[ON SCREEN: Word parts]

Crossing the sky to a smaller group of stars. I'll be cheering for you. Grete's tip about the front part is a good one. When a word starts with a beginning you didn't expect, it can change the whole meaning. So read every meaning all the way to the last word before you pick a root.


## Vocabulary · g4ela-16-3-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: suspicion]

Saying a meaning in your own words is the best test there is. If there's a grown-up nearby, try explaining suspicion to them right now. If you can do it without looking at the card, it's yours. Then try the same thing with the word that gives you the most trouble.


## Grammar · g4ela-16-3-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

No apostrophes at all today. That trap catches grown-ups all the time, so don't feel bad if it tricks you once. Before you type, ask whose. Then check the end of the sentence. If no thing comes right after the blank, you need the kind of owner-word that stands alone.


## Spelling · g4ela-16-3-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Don't guess by sound. That's the most important tip of the day. The related word on each line is there for a reason, so say it out loud before you type. Then try your spelling voice too, the one where every vowel is loud. Between the two, that hiding letter doesn't stand a chance.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-16-3-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 3]

Not walking past. That's a small choice, and it matters. It can feel easier not to ask about something sad or scary. But asking a grown-up you trust is often the brave thing. Think of a time you asked a hard question, even when part of you didn't want to know the answer.


## Before you read · g4ela-16-3-read

[ON SCREEN: Stop 2]

The door, the sign and a late visitor. Grete's giving you a lot to watch. Annemarie's Path shows where the shop is, right near the girls' building. As you read, think about what it means that this is happening on her own corner, not somewhere far away.


## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-16-3-rwm

The word something is doing a lot of quiet work in this one.

[QUOTE ON SCREEN: press 6, read the line, press 6 again]

The sentence comes from Chapter 3: "There was something frightening about his being here at night."

Notice the sentence ends with at night. The writer saves the time for last, so it lands. For your Mimic, end your sentence with the detail that makes it feel strange.


## Before you write · g4ela-16-3-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q1]

The door, the shops and Peter. Grete laid out the questions, so I'll add one reminder. When you describe the door, use the details from the chapter, the way a camera would see them. Save your feelings about it for after the description. Then say what the door tells you.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-16-3-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q3]

Thinking about Peter's visit as a clue is just right. A clue is something small that points to something bigger. So answer the first part with facts from the chapter. Then add a sentence about what the clue might be pointing to, even if you're not sure yet.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-16-3-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

Three reasons about three different things. That's what Sam does well. Check that yours don't all say the same idea in different words. If two reasons sound like twins, swap one for something new, like a different moment or a different thing Annemarie does. Read all three out loud to check.
