# Lesson 11.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 25 · Matthew Insists on Puffed Sleeves. Guide this lesson: Matthew. Each script plays right after Matthew's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-11-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week eleven. All week you've watched grown-ups who truly see Anne, first Mrs. Allan after the cake and then Miss Stacy. Chapter 25 gives Matthew his turn, and he has to walk into a store to do it. Your word work today is a review, so trust what you learned at the start of the week. After you read, you'll answer the chapter questions and write a rough ending for your story's first draft.

## Welcome · g4ela-11-4-welcome

Matthew calls it the hardest thing instead of the bravest, and I think for him those might be the same. Way back in Chapter 11, Anne wished for puffed sleeves, and today's chapter title tells you that wish comes back. Authors often plant a small wish early and answer it much later, after you've nearly forgotten it. Watch for that as you read Chapter 25.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-11-4-dol

Matthew claims he's no writer, but he spotted that run-on faster than plenty of grown-ups would. Now check the verbs. The model says Matthew will give, which points to the future. The sentence you're fixing uses unwrapped and watched, which tell what already happened. That's the same past and future tense from grammar this week. Read your fixed sentence back and make sure both halves stay in the past.

## Before the word work · g4ela-11-4-guide1

Poor Matthew, he can't even mention the store without blushing. You'll find out why soon enough. You already know every rule on this tab, because you worked them out yourself earlier in the week. Before you start the grammar or the spelling, try saying the rule to yourself in one sentence. If it won't come, peek at your notes. When you can say it plainly, the Show What You Know boxes get easier.

## Morphology · g4ela-11-4-morphology

Matthew says he's no scholar, but that cellar-stairs trick of his is a good one. Use the same test from earlier this week: cover the front chunk and look at what's left. Today two of the cards are roots, so that leftover piece gets a meaning of its own. The title of Chapter 21, A New Departure in Flavorings, holds a big word whose halves are both on these cards. Use it to check your thinking after you match.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-11-4-vocabulary

I believe Matthew completely. Explaining something out loud is one of the best ways to find out whether you really know it. You've seen these four words all week, so today you get to use them your way. Whichever choice you pick, try tying your word to a real moment from the book, like Anne on the Barry roof or the liniment cake. If the word fits that moment, you've got it.

## Grammar · g4ela-11-4-grammar

Matthew and Marilla disagreeing politely is about the most Green Gables thing I can picture. Earlier this week you learned that past tense doesn't always end in e-d, and that future tense pairs will with the plain verb. Keep both in mind, because these sentences retell the fall from the roof in Chapter 23. When you correct one in the box below, read the fixed sentence aloud. It should sound like a true retelling.

## Spelling · g4ela-11-4-spelling

I'd love to see Matthew squinting at a misspelled store sign. He'd never say a word, but he'd notice. Quick reminder from earlier this week: look at the letter just before the y, and then at the ending being added. Those two checks tell you which of the three cases you're in. The box at the bottom asks you to name the case, so explain it the way you'd tell a friend who missed this week.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-11-4-guide2

Matthew may not enjoy being noticed, but he's a careful noticer himself, and that matters today. At the start of the week, you learned that being seen isn't the same as being praised. Mrs. Allan saw Anne past the ruined cake. Today the person doing the seeing hardly talks at all. As you read, keep the box's question in mind, and watch who notices Anne without saying a word.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-11-4-wgrd

Matthew's already squirming just thinking about it, and I don't blame him one bit. You've been testing setting all week, from the tea at the manse to the party at Diana's. Today the box adds one more job: looking back across Chapters 21 to 25 to choose the chapter where Avonlea mattered most. Jot a note about the store while you read. Then compare it with the roof and the cake before you decide.

## Before you read · g4ela-11-4-read

I think Matthew knows more about why than he lets on. Think back to the very start of the book, when he drove to the station and found a girl waiting instead of a boy. He was shy then, and he still is. Keep that picture in your head, because it shows how big this errand is for him. Let that early chapter help you with the Pause and Think question.

## Before you write · g4ela-11-4-guide3

Matthew can laugh about that sugar now, and you're allowed to smile at it too. Two of today's questions want a quotation, so bring back the habit from earlier this week. Choose only the words that do the proving, put them in quotation marks, and follow them with a sentence of your own. Matthew says very little in this chapter, so his actions count as evidence too.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-11-4-chapter

Matthew's trying to talk you out of choosing him, which is exactly what a shy person would do. Same move from the start of the week: each question has two parts, so find the second half before you write. Question three asks how Anne changed, and that's the half people forget. Go back to the chapter for the adult you choose, find a moment right after they see her, and ask what Anne does differently.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-11-4-workshop

Matthew took his time too, and he still got there, so don't fret over making it perfect. Look at the box that says Anne did it too. A wish from Chapter 11 gets answered in Chapter 25, and your ending is where your kid's want from Scene 1 gets answered. In Now You Try, check that the trouble really climbed from Scene 1 up to Scene 3, your worst moment. Sam's ending is messy on purpose, and yours can be too.
