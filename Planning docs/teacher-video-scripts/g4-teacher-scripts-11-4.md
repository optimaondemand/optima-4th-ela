# Lesson 11.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 25 · Matthew Insists on Puffed Sleeves. Guide this lesson: Matthew. Each script plays right after Matthew's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-11-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week eleven. All week you've watched grown-ups who truly see Anne, first Mrs. Allan after the cake and then Miss Stacy. Chapter 25 gives Matthew his turn, and he has to walk into a store to do it. Your word work today is a review, so trust what you learned at the start of the week. After you read, you'll answer the chapter questions and write a rough ending for your story's first draft.

## Welcome · g4ela-11-4-welcome

Way back in Chapter 11, Anne wished for puffed sleeves, and the Growing-Up Watch box tells you that wish comes back today. Authors often plant a small wish early and answer it much later, after you've nearly forgotten it. Keep that idea close as you work through the page. Your own story gets its ending today, and an ending is where an early wish finally gets its answer.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-11-4-dol

Check the verbs in these two sentences. The model says Matthew will give, which points to the future. The sentence you're fixing uses unwrapped and watched, which tell what already happened. That's the same past and future tense you've practiced in grammar all week. Once you've fixed the sentence in your notebook, read it back and make sure both halves stay in the past.

## Before the word work · g4ela-11-4-guide1

You already know every rule on this tab, because you worked them out yourself earlier in the week. Today is the check. Before you start the grammar or the spelling, try saying the rule to yourself in one sentence. If it won't come, take a quick look back at your notes from earlier this week. When you can say it plainly, the Show What You Know boxes will feel much easier.

## Morphology · g4ela-11-4-morphology

Same test you used earlier this week: cover the front chunk and look at what's left. Today two of the cards are roots, so that leftover piece finally gets a meaning of its own. Think about the title of Chapter 21, A New Departure in Flavorings. Both halves of that big word are sitting on these cards. Use that title to check your thinking after you match.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-11-4-vocabulary

You've seen these four words in sentences and matches all week, so today you get to use them your way. If you choose the sentence about your realistic-fiction character, pick a word that truly fits that kid. Is your character vivacious? Was the moment in your Scene 3 precarious? Let the word do some of the describing for you, so your reader can picture it.

## Grammar · g4ela-11-4-grammar

Earlier this week you learned that past tense doesn't always end in e-d, and that future tense pairs will with the plain verb. Keep both in mind here, because these sentences retell the fall from the roof in Chapter 23, the day Diana watched it happen. When you correct one in the box below, read your fixed sentence aloud from start to finish. It should sound like a true retelling.

## Spelling · g4ela-11-4-spelling

Quick reminder from earlier this week: look at the letter just before the y, and then look at the ending being added. Those two checks tell you which of the three cases you're in. The box at the bottom asks you to name the case, so use your own words, as if you were explaining it to a friend who was out sick this week.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-11-4-guide2

At the start of the week, you learned that being seen isn't the same as being praised. Mrs. Allan saw Anne past the ruined cake. Today the person doing the seeing hardly talks at all. As you read, keep the question at the bottom of the box in mind, the one about who truly sees your character. In your story, it might be a quiet person too.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-11-4-wgrd

You've been testing setting all week, from the tea at the manse to the party at Diana's. Today the box adds one more job: looking back across Chapters 21 to 25 and choosing the chapter where Avonlea mattered most. Jot a quick note about the store and the small village as you read Chapter 25. Then compare it with the roof and the cake before you decide.

## Before you read · g4ela-11-4-read

Think back to the very beginning of the book, when Matthew drove to the station and found a girl waiting instead of a boy. He was shy then, and he still is. Keep that picture in your head today, because it shows you how big this errand is for him. When you reach the Pause and Think question, let that early chapter help you think it through.

## Before you write · g4ela-11-4-guide3

Two of today's questions want a quotation, so bring back the habit from earlier this week. Choose a short piece, only the words that do the proving, put them in quotation marks, and follow them with a sentence of your own about what they show. Matthew says very little in this chapter, so his actions count as evidence too. What he does can prove a point as well as any words.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-11-4-chapter

Same trick you learned at the start of the week: each of these questions has two parts, so find the second half before you write. Question three asks how Anne changed, and that's the half people forget. Go back to the chapter for the adult you choose and find a moment right after they see her. Then ask what Anne does differently from that point on.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-11-4-workshop

Look at the box that says Anne did it too. A wish from Chapter 11 finally gets answered in Chapter 25, and your ending is where your kid's want from Scene 1 gets answered. Earlier this week you built Scene 3, your worst moment. In Now You Try, check that the trouble really climbed from Scene 1 up to that moment. Sam's ending is messy on purpose, and yours can be too.
