# Lesson 11.3 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 24 · Miss Stacy and Her Pupils Get Up a Concert. Guide this lesson: Miss Stacy. Each script plays right after Miss Stacy's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-11-3

Hi, friends. Welcome to the third lesson of week eleven. So far this week, Mrs. Allan saw past a ruined cake, and Anne got a letter addressed to Miss Anne Shirley. Today, in Chapter 24, a new teacher comes to the Avonlea school. Your word work is mostly building, so you'll use what you practiced in the last two lessons on your own. Then you'll read, borrow one of the author's sentence shapes, and write Scene 4.

## Welcome · g4ela-11-3-welcome

I'm a teacher too, so I know how Miss Stacy feels. We'd better be ready for a question like that. This whole week has been about being seen. You already watched Mrs. Allan notice Anne on a bad day. Miss Stacy is brand new, so as you read, pay attention to how fast Anne makes up her mind about her.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-11-3-dol

Miss Stacy sounds pleased to find her own schoolroom in a sentence, and I don't blame her. You already know the run-on test from the start of the week. Find the chunks that could each stand alone. The verbs give you one more clue. When the time in a sentence suddenly jumps, a new sentence often begins right there. Compare your fix with the model before you check.

## Before the word work · g4ela-11-3-guide1

Better than Miss Stacy? I doubt that, but she's right to trust your ear. In the first lesson you sorted, and in the second you hunted for the words that broke the pattern. Today the page hands you the whole job. Each activity still has its reminder box at the top and a hint button. Try each one yourself first, then the hint, and only then press Check My Work.

## Morphology · g4ela-11-3-morphology

I love that Miss Stacy takes her class outside to study ferns. That kind of slow looking works on words, too. You've met anti- and de- twice this week, so use the test from earlier: a real prefix comes off and leaves some meaning behind. When you finish, try one bonus word in your head. A train can derail. What does that first chunk tell you happened?

## Vocabulary · g4ela-11-3-vocabulary

A line from the book hidden in the activity? That's a fun surprise to watch for. These are the same four words you've matched all week. Today they sit inside sentences about people from the book. Think about what you already know about each person. Then check that the word's meaning fits the person, and not only the sound of the sentence.

## Grammar · g4ela-11-3-grammar

That yesterday trick of Miss Stacy's is a good one, and I use it myself. You learned this week that past tense tells what already happened and future tense tells what hasn't happened yet. Now you make the verbs yourself. After the blanks, the Show What You Know box asks for both times in one sentence. Think about what a reader learns about Anne from having both.

## Spelling · g4ela-11-3-spelling

Sketching a leaf before drawing it properly sounds just like Miss Stacy, careful and patient. You've worked with this y rule since the first lesson. Today the Show What You Know box asks for a y word from Chapter 24, so jot one down when you spot it while you read. Then come back and build it. Explaining why you spelled it that way shows you truly understand.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-11-3-guide2

You've only just met Miss Stacy, and already her care with names tells you a lot about her. Earlier this week, in Chapter 22, Anne opened a letter addressed to Miss Anne Shirley and was thrilled. Today the thing that matters is even smaller, just one letter at the end of her name. You know how much that e has mattered to her since her very first day at Green Gables.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-11-3-wgrd

Counting heads in a room is such a teacher thing to suggest. Miss Stacy notices everyone. In the last lesson, a garden full of girls, Josie Pye among them, helped push Anne onto a roof. Today the crowd is different, first a class and then a whole audience. A crowd makes some people braver and others shyer. As you read, decide which kind Anne is, and whether you'd be the same.

## Before you read · g4ela-11-3-read

Miss Stacy promises not to listen, which is polite of her, though I suspect she'd like to. This chapter starts a little while after the roof. Anne has been home healing, so notice what she's been missing at school while she was away. That will help you understand why her first days back feel so big to her.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-11-3-rwm

Miss Stacy is a little embarrassed by this one, and I think that's sweet. Most of us blush when someone describes us. Earlier this week you saw how one word, like lazy in front of shadows, can carry a whole feeling. This sentence stacks describing words to introduce a person. For your Mimic, choose a real person, like a grandparent or a neighbor. A true detail beats a fancy one every time.

## Before you write · g4ela-11-3-guide3

Compositions is the old word for pieces of writing, and I like how grand it sounds. Check how many parts each question has before you start, the same habit you used earlier this week. The first two questions each ask for two things. The third asks about two groups of people, so make sure both get a fair turn in your answer.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-11-3-chapter

It's generous of Miss Stacy to admit the careful grown-ups have a point. Not every teacher would. Question three reaches back to Chapter 22, when Mrs. Lynde called a lady teacher a dangerous innovation. An innovation is a brand new way of doing something. So think about what's new in Miss Stacy's schoolroom compared with the school Marilla and Mrs. Lynde would remember.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-11-3-workshop

Once there's a concert to practice for, nobody can keep Anne still, and Miss Stacy saw that right away. In the last lesson you built the worst moment and slowed the clock down. Scene 4 comes right after it. Look at Sam's draft. Diego offers a hand, but every action after that belongs to Mateo. Check your own actions the same way. If someone else is doing the hard part, give it back to your kid.
