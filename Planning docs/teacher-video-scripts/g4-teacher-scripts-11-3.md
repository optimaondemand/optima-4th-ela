# Lesson 11.3 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 24 · Miss Stacy and Her Pupils Get Up a Concert. Guide this lesson: Miss Stacy. Each script plays right after Miss Stacy's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-11-3

Hi, friends. Welcome to the third lesson of week eleven. So far this week, Mrs. Allan saw past a ruined cake, and Anne got a letter addressed to Miss Anne Shirley. Today, in Chapter 24, a new teacher comes to the Avonlea school. Your word work is mostly building, so you'll use what you practiced in the last two lessons on your own. Then you'll read, borrow one of the author's sentence shapes, and write Scene 4.

## Welcome · g4ela-11-3-welcome

This whole week has been about being seen, and today the person doing the seeing is a teacher. You already watched Mrs. Allan notice Anne on a bad day. Miss Stacy is brand new, so as you read, pay attention to how fast Anne makes up her mind about her. Think about a teacher or coach who made you feel noticed, and hold onto that feeling while you read.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-11-3-dol

You already know the run-on test from the start of the week: find the chunks that could each stand alone. Today the verbs give you an extra clue. You've been learning that will points ahead in time, while past verbs tell what's already done. When the time in a sentence suddenly jumps, that's often a sign a new sentence should begin. Compare your fix with the model before you check.

## Before the word work · g4ela-11-3-guide1

In the first lesson you sorted, and in the second you hunted for the words that broke the pattern. Today the page hands the whole job to you. You aren't on your own, though. Each activity still has its little reminder box at the top and a hint button for when you're stuck. Try each one yourself first, then the hint, and only then press Check My Work.

## Morphology · g4ela-11-3-morphology

You've met anti- and de- twice already this week, so this is your chance to show they've stuck. Use the test from earlier: a real prefix comes off and leaves some meaning behind. The examples box has antifreeze and depart if you want a quick look back. When you finish, try one bonus word in your head. A train can derail. What does that front chunk tell you happened?

## Vocabulary · g4ela-11-3-vocabulary

These are the same four words you've been matching all week, so the meanings should feel familiar by now. Today they sit inside sentences about people you know from the book. That's a real help. Before you choose, think about the person in each sentence and what you already know about them. Then check that the word's meaning really fits the person, and not only the sound of the sentence.

## Grammar · g4ela-11-3-grammar

You learned this week that past tense tells what already happened and future tense tells what hasn't happened yet. Now you make the verbs yourself. Every one of these sentences is about something you've already read, so let the story help you. After the blanks, the Show What You Know box asks for both times in one sentence. Think about what a reader learns about Anne from having both.

## Spelling · g4ela-11-3-spelling

You've worked with this y rule since the first lesson, first sorting it and then catching the exceptions. Today you put it all together. The Show What You Know box asks for a y word from Chapter 24, so while you read, jot one down when you spot it. Then come back and build it. Explaining why you spelled it that way is the part that shows you truly understand.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-11-3-guide2

This box connects to something from earlier this week. In Chapter 22, Anne opened a letter addressed to Miss Anne Shirley and was thrilled. Today the thing that matters is even smaller, just one letter at the end of her name. You know how much that e has mattered to her since her very first day at Green Gables. Take a minute with the question at the bottom of the box about your own character.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-11-3-wgrd

This is the same reading move from the last lesson, when Anne was at a party with Josie Pye watching. So you already know that a setting includes the people in it. What's new today is a teacher, and then a whole audience. A crowd makes some people braver and others shyer. As you read, decide which kind Anne is, and whether you'd be the same.

## Before you read · g4ela-11-3-read

You know how Pause and Think works by now. Read both questions before you open the book so they're waiting in your mind. This chapter starts a little while after the roof. Anne has been home healing, so notice what she's been missing at school while she was away. That will help you understand why her first days back feel so big to her.

## Read Like a Writer · g4ela-11-3-rwm

Earlier this week you saw how one word, like lazy in front of shadows, can carry a whole feeling. This sentence uses describing words too, but it stacks them up to introduce a person. For your Mimic, choose a real person, like a grandparent or a neighbor. Then name the gift you'd honestly notice after an afternoon with them. A true detail beats a fancy one every time.

## Before you write · g4ela-11-3-guide3

Same habit as the start of the week: check how many parts each question has before you write. The first two questions each ask for two things. The third asks about two groups of people, so make sure both get a fair turn in your answer. And if you see an extra challenge line under a question, give it a try, just like before.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-11-3-chapter

Question three reaches back to Chapter 22, when Mrs. Lynde called a lady teacher a dangerous innovation. An innovation is a brand new way of doing something. So think about what's new in Miss Stacy's schoolroom compared with the school Marilla and Mrs. Lynde would remember. As you did earlier this week, put quotation marks around the words you borrow, then add a sentence of your own explaining them.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-11-3-workshop

In the last lesson you built the worst moment of your story and slowed the clock right down. Scene 4 comes right after it. Look at Sam's draft. Diego offers a hand, but every action after that belongs to Mateo. Check your own three actions the same way and ask whose they are. If someone else is doing the hard part, give it back to your kid.
