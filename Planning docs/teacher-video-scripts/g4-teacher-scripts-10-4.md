# Lesson 10.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 20 · A Good Imagination Gone Wrong. Guide this lesson: Marilla. Each script is about 30 to 60 seconds and is meant to be recorded where it plays on the page.

## Lesson video · g4ela-10-4

Hi, friends. This is the last lesson of week ten, and it's a good one. All week, Anne has meant one thing while the grown-ups around her saw something else. At the tea party, on the night Minnie May was sick, and again today. In Chapter 20, the thing that gets Anne into trouble is her own imagination, the very thing we've loved about her since she stepped off the train. Today you'll also wrap up your word work for the week. These are review sorts, which means some of what you see is already correct, so read carefully before you tap. At the end, you'll look back at the whole week and think about how much Anne has changed.

## Welcome · g4ela-10-4-welcome

Welcome back. Today's chapter is called "A Good Imagination Gone Wrong," and that title is worth thinking about before you start. We usually think of imagination as a good thing, and for Anne it mostly is. It's how she turns a pond into the Lake of Shining Waters. But today you'll see what happens when something she made up stops feeling like a game. As you work, keep asking yourself this: can the same thing be someone's greatest gift and also their biggest problem? You'll see that idea in the chapter, and you'll also use it in your own writing at the end of the lesson.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-10-4-dol

Today's sentences have someone talking, so they need quotation marks. Look at the model first. Notice that the quotation marks go around only the words Marilla actually says, and the comma sits inside the closing quotation mark, right before the word "said." That comma is the part people forget most often. Now look at the sentence you're fixing. Read it out loud and ask yourself, "Which words would come out of Marilla's mouth?" Those are the words that go inside the quotation marks. Then check every little "i." When "I" is a word all by itself, it's always a capital. And Marilla is a name, so she gets a capital too.

## Before the word work · g4ela-10-4-guide1

This is the last day of word work for the week, so today is all about review. Two of these activities are checking activities. That means some of the sentences or words are already correct, and your job is to find only the ones that aren't. Tapping a correct one counts against you, so slow down. Read each item all the way through before you touch it. A good habit is to say it out loud in your head and ask, "Does this sound right, and does it look right?" Those are two different questions, especially in spelling. For the vocabulary, you'll get to choose one activity from a menu. Choose the one you can finish well.

## Morphology · g4ela-10-4-morphology

By now you know these four prefixes all mean "not." Today's job is matching each prefix to the rule for when to use it. That rule depends on the first letter of the word that comes after the prefix. Read the meanings slowly, because they look very similar. The difference between them is which letters each prefix comes before, so that's where your eyes should go. If you get stuck, take one of the example words and pull the prefix off. For "impossible," take off the i-m and look at what's left. What letter does "possible" start with? That letter is your clue to which rule belongs to which prefix.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-10-4-vocabulary

Today you get to choose how you show what you know about this week's words. There are several choices, and you only do one. Read them all once, then pick, and don't spend too long deciding. Choose the one you can finish well. If you pick the one about explaining a word to a younger student, use simple, everyday words in your explanation, and give an example a younger kid would understand. If you use two words in a sentence about your story character, make sure the words actually fit that person. A sentence can be grammatically fine and still use a word the wrong way. Read your sentence back and ask whether it makes sense.

## Grammar · g4ela-10-4-grammar

This is a review, so some of these sentences are already correct. You're only tapping the ones where the verb doesn't match its subject. For each sentence, find who or what is doing the action first. Then ask: is that one person, or more than one? Then look at the end of the verb. Be extra careful with the longer sentences. When there are a few words between the subject and the verb, it's easy to match the verb to the wrong word. In a phrase like "the cups on the table," the subject is "cups," not "table." Read each sentence out loud before you decide. The wrong ones usually sound a little off.

## Spelling · g4ela-10-4-spelling

This spelling review is tricky, because a misspelled word can sound exactly right when you say it. "Sellar" with an s sounds just like "cellar" with a c. So for this one, your ears alone won't get you there. You also have to use the rule you worked on this week. Look at the first letter of each word and the letter right after it, and ask whether that's how the rule says to spell that sound. If a word sounds right and the letters follow the rule, leave it alone. If it sounds right but the letters are wrong, that's the one to tap. Go slowly, because tapping a correct word counts against you.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-10-4-guide2

Before you read, look back at the Growing-Up Watch box. This week's trait has been courage, and the box reminds you of the night Anne saved Minnie May. She was afraid, but she knew what to do, and she did it. That's one kind of courage. In today's chapter, you'll see Anne afraid again, but this time she's afraid of something she made up herself. As you read, think about whether facing that kind of fear takes courage too. Then read the What Good Readers Do box right below this one, because it gives you an important job to do while you're reading today.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-10-4-wgrd

This week you've been comparing perspectives, and today you're taking it one step further. Notice that this story almost always keeps you inside Anne's head. You hear her thoughts and feel her fears. You rarely hear what Marilla is thinking. That makes it easy to be on Anne's side every time. So today, try retelling one moment from Marilla's point of view. She doesn't see a frightened girl and a haunted wood. She sees something else. Once you've tried that, look at the zoom-out question at the bottom. Think back over the whole week, from the tea party to today, and notice whose side you were kept on each time.

## Before you read · g4ela-10-4-read

There's just one place to stop today, at the end of the chapter. The question asks how an imagination can be both a gift and a problem, and what Anne is learning. While you're reading, pay attention to when the Haunted Wood was invented and who made it up. Then notice how Anne feels about it at the beginning compared to later on. When you get to the walk, slow down and notice the details she imagines. Those details come from her own mind, not from the wood itself. And when you think about what Anne is learning, remember that learning something and being told something aren't always the same thing.

## Before you write · g4ela-10-4-guide3

This is the work you'll turn in, and today's questions ask you to look back over the whole week, not just one chapter. So it's a good idea to think back to Chapter 16 before you start writing. The second question asks you to take a side about Marilla, and it's okay to have a strong opinion. Just make sure you can point to something in the chapter that supports it. Don't base your answer on what you would have done. Base it on what Marilla said and did, and what happened because of it. If you're not sure about a detail, open your book and look it up. That's what good readers do.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-10-4-chapter

Let's talk about these three questions before you write. For the first one, the hint tells you the Haunted Wood was invented in daylight. Think about what's different at night, and also about what's going on inside Anne's head. The second question asks if Marilla is being fair or cruel. Either answer can work, but you need evidence. Think about what Marilla was trying to teach, and whether Anne actually learned it. The third question asks you to compare Anne at the beginning of the week and at the end. Think about how she felt after losing Diana, and how she feels after the walk. What's different about her? Use details from both chapters.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-10-4-workshop

Today you aren't writing new scenes. You're reading back what you have. Read your two scenes from the top, out loud if you can. When you stumble over a sentence, that's a sign it needs fixing, so fix it now. Then check your word count. After that, think about your main character's gift, the thing that makes them special. Look at what Sam did. Mateo's gift is that he cares so much, and that's also what makes him want to quit. Anne is the same way. Her imagination is what we love about her, and it's what got her into trouble today. So find the one quality that could both help and hurt your character.
