# Lesson 17.4 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 4 · Ch. 8, There Has Been a Death. Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 40 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment: click a button in it and that card goes up on your video. It is never read aloud.

## Welcome · g4ela-17-4-welcome

A relative nobody has ever mentioned. That sent a little shiver down my back. This chapter is full of sunshine, cream and a kitten named after thunder, but the grown-ups keep saying things that don't quite fit. Read it like a detective. Every time a grown-up says something odd, jot it down.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-17-4-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

Weighing courage is hard work, so make it simple. Write the name of each brave person in your journal. Next to each name, write one thing that person could lose. Then read your list from top to bottom and compare. A grown-up and a child might both be brave, but what they risk can look very different.


## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-17-4-dol

[ON SCREEN: Sentence B · Show the fix]

A name with a title in front. I'll show you a different sentence with the same name in it. A title used as part of someone's name stands tall, just like the name does, and a little joining mark holds the two title words together. Then split the two thoughts. Now try yours.


## Before the word work · g4ela-17-4-guide1

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule · Spelling rule]

Knowing when to leave something alone is a real editor's skill. Before you tap, say out loud what's wrong. If you can't name the problem, it might be just fine. Leaving a correct sentence alone is a right answer, too. Good editors circle back once at the end, just to be sure.


## Morphology · g4ela-17-4-morphology

[ON SCREEN: Word parts]

The last stretch of water. We made it. Grete's question about believing a story is a good one to keep. Annemarie hears a story in this chapter, and she doesn't swallow it whole. When the beam finally reaches the far shore, watch the lighthouse on the other side.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-17-4-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: discreet · tense]

No misses at all? You can do it. Then take Grete's challenge. Discreet and tense really do make a good pair for this chapter. Think about how Annemarie feels when she hears strange news, and what she chooses not to say. Write your sentence. Then read it out loud and check that each word really fits.


## Grammar · g4ela-17-4-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

Your ear usually knows first. That's so true. Trust it, and then check the rule card to be sure. Read each sentence twice, once in your head and once in a whisper. Fix one sentence well rather than rushing through all of them. When you explain your fix, say what you heard that tipped you off.


## Spelling · g4ela-17-4-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Six words, and only some are wrong. Checking a built word is like checking a bike chain one link at a time. Check the front piece, then the base, then the end. The broken link is easy to spot once you separate them. And leave the words that are already right alone.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-17-4-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 4]

A child who notices everything and says nothing. That's Annemarie in this chapter. Watch for the moment she decides to stay quiet, and ask yourself why she chooses silence. Is staying quiet a kind of courage? Hold on to your answer, because the questions after the chapter will ask you to use what you noticed.


## Before you read · g4ela-17-4-read

[ON SCREEN: Stop 3]

A fisherman who talks about the weather. I'm already suspicious. At breakfast, Annemarie makes a joke with relocate, one of last week's words. Find it and smile. Near the end, read what Uncle Henrik says about fishing twice. Have you heard words like those before?


## Before you write · g4ela-17-4-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q2]

An odd phrase about fishing. Go back to the phone call in Chapter 6 and find what Papa asked. Put the two lines side by side. You don't have to know the whole answer yet. A careful guess with evidence is enough. Thinking out loud on paper is what good readers do.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-17-4-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q1]

A good guess backed up by what you noticed. That's real reading. For the first question, go back to the last page. Annemarie lists more than one reason the news seems strange. Find each one and use them all. Put them in your own words, then add the detail you think matters most.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-17-4-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

Reason, evidence, link, one more time. You know this shape now. The hard part is finding a second reason that's truly new. Look in a different chapter from your first one. A new chapter usually gives you a new moment and a new quote. If your two paragraphs still sound alike, try writing about a different person's courage.
