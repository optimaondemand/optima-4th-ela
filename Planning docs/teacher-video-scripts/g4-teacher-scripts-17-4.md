# Lesson 17.4 teacher video scripts

Number the Stars, Day 4 · Ch. 8, There Has Been a Death. Guide this lesson: Grete, the narrator. Each script plays right after Grete's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment: click a button in it and that card goes up on your video. It is never read aloud.

## Welcome · g4ela-17-4-welcome

Something isn't quite right. Hold on to that feeling. This chapter is full of sunshine, cream and kittens, but the grown-ups keep saying things that don't fit. Read it like a detective.


## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-17-4-wgrd

[ON SCREEN: Reading move]

Weighing courage is hard, so make it simple. Write each brave person's name, and next to it, one thing they could lose. Then compare your list.

Here's why Great-Aunt gets capitals and a hyphen here. It's part of her name, like a title, so it stands tall. The hyphen hooks great and aunt into one word.


## Before the word work · g4ela-17-4-guide1

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule · Spelling rule]

Knowing when to leave something alone is a real editor's skill. Before you tap, say why it's wrong. If you can't name the problem, it might be fine.


## Morphology · g4ela-17-4-morphology

[ON SCREEN: Word parts]

Credulous is a fun one. It means someone who believes things too easily. As you play, think about Annemarie. Is she credulous about Great-aunt Birte? Not at all. When the beam finally reaches the far shore, watch the lighthouse on the other side.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-17-4-vocabulary

[ON SCREEN: discreet · tense]

Discreet and tense really do make a good pair today. Annemarie feels tense when she hears the strange news, but she stays discreet. She doesn't say a word. Try that sentence your own way.


## Grammar · g4ela-17-4-grammar

[ON SCREEN: Grammar rule]

Trust your ear first, then check the rule. With he, she or it, the verb gets an s. With they, it doesn't. Fix one sentence carefully rather than rushing through all of them.


## Spelling · g4ela-17-4-spelling

[ON SCREEN: Spelling rule]

Taking a word apart is like checking a bike chain one link at a time. Check the front piece, then the base, then the end. The broken link is usually easy to spot once you separate them.


## Before you open the book · g4ela-17-4-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 4]

A child who notices everything and says nothing. That's Annemarie in this chapter. Watch for the moment she decides to stay quiet, and ask yourself why.


## Before you read · g4ela-17-4-read

Here's a fun moment to look for at breakfast. Annemarie makes a joke using relocate, one of last week's words. Find it and smile. Then, near the end, Uncle Henrik says something about fishing. Read it twice.


## Before you write · g4ela-17-4-guide3

[ON SCREEN: Q2]

For the fishing question, go back to Chapter 6 and find what Papa asked on the telephone. Put the two lines side by side. You don't have to know the whole answer yet. A careful guess with evidence is enough.


## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-17-4-chapter

[ON SCREEN: Q1]

For the first question, the book gives Annemarie three reasons to be puzzled. No one called, no one seemed sad, and she'd never heard the name. Find all three and use them.


## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-17-4-workshop

[ON SCREEN: Today’s focus]

To find your second reason, look in a different chapter from your first one. A new chapter usually gives you a new moment and a new quote.
