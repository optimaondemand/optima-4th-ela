# Lesson 20.4 teacher video scripts

Day 4 · Ch. 4, The Train Whistle Blows. Freedom Train. Guide this lesson: Marcus (college history student, former walking-tour guide on the Eastern Shore). Each script plays right after Marcus's clip in the same spot, so it adds to what he says instead of repeating it. About 40 to 60 seconds each. A gold 🎬 On screen line is a suggestion for that moment; it is never read aloud.

## Lesson video · g4ela-20-4

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

Hi, friends. In Chapter 4, Harriet is fifteen, and Sterling tells us how strong she has become. You'll meet a young man named Jim who tells her about trains: a real steam train he saw in Baltimore, and another kind of railroad that isn't really a railroad at all. After you read, open the songs card for the song Jim sings in the barn. This chapter ends with a frightening moment, so start it when you have time to finish. In Writer's Workshop, you'll plan where to look for your research answers.


## Welcome · g4ela-20-4-welcome

It isn't only about riding. Here's what I'd add. In this chapter, a train is also an idea: a way out, a way north. When Jim talks about the secret railroad, listen to the words he borrows, like station and conductor. Those words come from real railroads, but here they're used for a hidden purpose. When you write your central idea, see if you can explain how one idea, a train, can mean two different things.


## Freedom Watch · g4ela-20-4-guide2

[ON SCREEN: Stop 1]

Hearing about freedom before you can reach it. Harriet doesn't say much in this chapter, but her heart says a lot. Watch for the moment Sterling tells us her heart is pounding, and ask yourself why. Then think about a time you heard about something that seemed almost impossible, and it stayed with you for days. Write one sentence in your journal about that, and one sentence about what Harriet might be hoping for.


## Before you read · g4ela-20-4-read

[ON SCREEN: Chapter picture]

A gentle warning before you read: the end of this chapter is frightening. Harriet is badly hurt while helping someone else. Sterling tells it quickly, and the chapter stops right after, so you'll be left wondering. That's on purpose. If it upsets you, it's okay to talk with a grown-up about it. Before you get there, slow down for Jim's stories and the song in the barn. They help explain the choice Harriet makes.
