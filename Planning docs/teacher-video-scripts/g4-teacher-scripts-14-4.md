# Lesson 14.4 teacher video scripts

Anne of Green Gables, Ch. 38 · The Bend in the Road. Guide this lesson: Anne. Each script plays right after Anne's audio clip in the same spot, so it adds to what she says instead of repeating it. About 20 to 30 seconds each, since this is a review day.

## Lesson video · g4ela-14-4

Hi, friends. This is it, the last chapter of Anne of Green Gables. Earlier this week Anne spent a winter at Queen's, won the Avery scholarship, and then lost Matthew. Today, in Chapter 38, she comes to a bend in her road. Your word work reviews the roots, helping verbs, and suffix rules you practiced in the first two lessons, so trust what you know. Then you'll read, look back across the whole book, and share your finished story.

## Welcome · g4ela-14-4-welcome

The model sentence in today's Daily Oral Language comes from Mrs. Rachel Lynde. Think back to the very first chapter, when she sat at her window wondering where Matthew was driving, and thought taking in an orphan was a bad idea. Now, in the last chapter, she's the one bringing Anne good news. Authors often circle back to where a story started. While you read, watch for other familiar faces and how they treat Anne now.

## What Good Readers Do · g4ela-14-4-wgrd

You already practiced this move at the start of the week, with the frame on this page, "Anne used to ___, and now she ___." Keep both blanks as things Anne actually does. What's new today is the size of it. The Think About It question stretches from the train station in Chapter 2 to the gate in Chapter 38. Give the part about what never changed real thought, because that half is often the more interesting one.

## Daily Oral Language · g4ela-14-4-dol

You've been finding helpers all week, so this model should feel familiar. In the trustees' sentence, have decided tells you the deciding is already done. Now look at the sentence you're fixing. Its two halves sit at different times again, one finished and one still ahead, and you already know what that usually means for where a sentence should stop. When you're done, read your version aloud and listen for the pause.

## Before the word work · g4ela-14-4-guide1

Everything on this tab comes from the week you just had. The roots, the four words, the helping verbs, and the suffix rules all showed up in the first two lessons. ## Morphology · g4ela-14-4-morphology

You've met these two roots in both earlier lessons this week, from the projector on the wall to the manuscript written by hand. Today's match puts the small front pieces back in. If one gives you trouble, look at the examples box on this page, because each example word already shows its parts split apart. The writing root fits Anne's ending, too. She's about to stand in front of a classroom of her own.

## Vocabulary · g4ela-14-4-vocabulary

You've already worked with ambition, consolation, and sanctifying this week. Obstinate is the one we haven't talked about yet. It means stubborn, and Anne has been obstinate before, like all those years she wouldn't forgive Gilbert. If you choose the card about finding a word in Chapter 38, keep ambition in mind as you read. Think about what Anne decides to do with her big plans in this chapter.

## Grammar · g4ela-14-4-grammar

You've had two lessons of practice with this. Find the main verb first, then look to its left for a helper. And keep in mind what we saw earlier this week, that a verb like has or had can sometimes stand on its own as the main verb. So a sentence can be short and still be complete. When you fix one in Show What You Know, say what time the helper puts the action in.

## Spelling · g4ela-14-4-spelling

You already know the reasons behind these three rules, like the double letter that guards a short vowel sound. That reason matters most today, because the Show What You Know box asks how you'll remember the rule. Saying why a rule works is usually the best memory trick there is. Try the finger trick from earlier this week on each of the six words, and trust what it shows you.

## Before you open the book · g4ela-14-4-guide2

At the start of the week, this box talked about bitterness, and the grudge Anne held against Gilbert for years. Today's box brings him back one last time. Look at the question at the bottom about your own character. You don't need to change your finished story. Just notice whether your character faced a turn they didn't plan, because that might be worth mentioning in your reflection later today.

## Before you read · g4ela-14-4-read

This is the final chapter, and Matthew's death in the chapter before still hangs over Green Gables, so read it gently. Two names to know. Redmond is a college, the next big step Anne had planned after Queen's. Carmody is a nearby town with its own school. The Pause and Think question at the end asks about your own road, and there's nothing to write, so give it a real minute before moving on.

## Before you write · g4ela-14-4-guide3

For your answers today, use what you've practiced since the first lesson this week. A then sentence and a now sentence, joined by a word like but or instead, makes a change easy to see. And since helping verbs have been our grammar all week, read your answers once just for verbs. Check that the past stays in the past and Anne's plans for the future sound like the future.

## Chapter Thinking · g4ela-14-4-chapter

Each question has a challenge that pushes a little further. For the first one, the challenge asks about Anne's own words on sacrifice, so look for what she tells Marilla, and use her reason instead of yours. For the second, think back to the slate in Chapter 15 and put that day beside the gate. For the third, your thinking from What Good Readers Do earlier on this page is already a strong start.

## Writer's Workshop · g4ela-14-4-workshop

Sam's after reflection works for the same reason the rubric did in our second lesson. He gives proof, a real example from his story about Mateo. When you reflect on your growth as a writer, look back at the rubric row you chose to improve this week. That's an easy place to find your example, because you know exactly what changed. And pick a listener who'll stay with you all the way to your ending.
