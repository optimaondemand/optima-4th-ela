## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-welcome.mp3` — Warm-Up (in the greeting)

> I’m Miss Stacy. This chapter begins with a visit I paid to Green Gables, and it ends on the last day of a school year. Your question is about something Anne loses, and I’ll be honest, I can’t tell you what it is. A teacher sees a great deal, but not everything. First there’s word work, and some of it is already correct. Then read, and watch closely.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-dol.mp3` — Warm-Up · Daily Oral Language

> The top sentence is about me, and I remember that day well. Look at what it does right. I count four things, and one of them is a comma doing a quiet job. Find all four before you tap. The bottom sentence runs two thoughts together. Find where the first one ends. Then look for a word that names a language. It wants a capital, and so does the girl.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-guide1.mp3` — Word Study tab

> Review day, and two of these are traps. In the grammar and the spelling, some words are right and some are wrong, and tapping a right one costs you. So slow down. Read each word as if you were marking a composition. Then the vocabulary gives you a choice of what to write. Choose the one that will make you think hardest. That’s usually the one worth doing.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-morphology.mp3` — Word Study · Morphology

> Matching once more. Two roots, two front parts, and four meanings to pair them with. You’ve had a week with these, so trust yourself. If one won’t come, think of a whole word with that part inside and say it aloud. On our field afternoons I never let anyone name a fern until they had looked at it properly. Look first, then tap.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-vocabulary.mp3` — Word Study · Vocabulary

> The last day for this week’s four words, and you choose how to show you know them. You can write sentences, explain the parts, hunt for a word in today’s chapter that comes close, or teach one to someone younger. I like the teaching one best. You never really know a word until you’ve had to explain it to a small person with a great many questions.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-grammar.mp3` — Word Study · Grammar

> A short draft, and some of the doing words are wrong. Not all of them. Tap only the ones a careful writer would fix, and leave the good ones alone. Say each sentence aloud and listen for the word that makes you wince. Then, in the box, tell me what the writer was reaching for. Mistakes usually make sense from the inside. That’s what makes them worth studying.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-spelling.mp3` — Word Study · Spelling

> Six words, and some are spelled wrongly. Tap only those. For each one, ask whether the quiet e is doing its job. Is it there when the sound needs it? Is it missing when the next letter can do the work instead? Then, in the box, mend one and tell me what would happen to the sound without that e. Say it both ways. You’ll hear it.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-guide2.mp3` — Reading tab

> There’s a box here about learning from mistakes. I see a good many in a schoolroom, and I’ve come to think the mistake matters less than what happens after. When a pupil tells me the truth about one, I forgive it, and we don’t speak of it again. As you read, watch what Anne carries with her this year, and what she finds she has let go.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-wgrd.mp3` — Reading · What Good Readers Do

> The box asks you to zoom out. Not one chapter, but the whole week, in two sentences. That means most of what happened has to go. I ask my older pupils to do the same with a year of history, and it hurts at first. Keep only what still matters at the end. If a moment changed nothing later, let it go, however much you enjoyed it.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-read.mp3` — Reading · Read: Ch. 30

> One stop, at the very end. By then the year is over, and something has changed in Anne that I didn’t see. Nobody did, I think. Think about what it costs her, and why she keeps it to herself. Then think about what it tells you about how she’s growing up. You’ll know more about it than I do. That’s the privilege of a reader.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-guide3.mp3` — Assignment tab

> Now it’s your turn, and I’ll tell you what I tell my Queen’s class: don’t tell me, show me. For the first two questions, stay inside this chapter and put your finger on the line. The last one looks back across the whole week, and that takes a steadier hand. Anne would pick five mistakes and follow all of them. Pick one, and follow it to what it taught.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-chapter.mp3` — Assignment · Chapter Thinking

> The first question asks what my new class is for, and who joined it. Count them all, and notice who isn’t there. The second asks about Anne and Gilbert. I can tell you they work very hard to beat each other. The rest the book knows, and I don’t. The third asks you to look back over the whole week and choose one mistake.

## `stacy\g4ela-12-4-workshop.mp3` — Assignment · Writer’s Workshop

> Today, don’t fix anything. Read your whole story from the first line to the last, the way a stranger would. Then answer two questions. Which part is strongest, and which part still needs work? Name the scene. Name the sentence. Anne takes stock in this chapter of where she is and where she’s going. At the end of a year, so should a writer.
