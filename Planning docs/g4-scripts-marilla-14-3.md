## Her voice

Real lines from the book:

- “There—there—don’t cry so, dearie. It can’t bring him back.”
- “We’ve got each other, Anne. I don’t know what I’d do if you weren’t here—if you’d never come.”
- “It’s never been easy for me to say things out of my heart, but at times like this it’s easier.” (all Ch. 37)

**How she sounds:** Plain and steady, never sentimental. She speaks of Matthew directly, once, and keeps to ordinary work as the thing that holds you up. The RWM clip is hers to own: she is the one who “crept in.” She doesn’t know what the oculist will say (that’s Ch. 38).

The opening paragraph under the greeting is also in this voice (text only, 64 words):

> It’s Marilla. This is a hard chapter, and I’ll not pretend otherwise. Matthew was my brother, and the kindest man in Avonlea. Read it at whatever pace you need. If you have to stop and come back to it, that’s allowed. Anne and I got through it together. If it sits heavy with you, tell a grown-up you trust. There’s no weakness in that.

## The clips, in page order

### welcome · `g4ela-14-3-welcome.mp3` · 59 words

It’s Marilla. Today’s question is about grief, and how it moves. I’ll be plain, the way I always am. Matthew died, and this chapter is about the days after. Anne couldn’t cry at first. Then she could. There’s word work to do first, and that’s no bad thing. It steadies the hands. Then the chapter. Go gently with it.

### guide1 · `g4ela-14-3-guide1.mp3` · 63 words

Word work first. It’s ordinary work, and I’ll tell you something about ordinary work. After Matthew, the cows still needed milking and the bread still needed baking, and I found the doing of it held me up. So take this the same way. One blank at a time, one word at a time. Do each one properly, and the next one will come.

### guide2 · `g4ela-14-3-guide2.mp3` · 65 words

There’s a box here about being the one who carries it. Anne carried a great deal those first days. There were neighbors in and out, and there was me, and she kept herself steady for all of us. I didn’t see what it cost her until later. Read, and watch what she does in front of people, and what she saves for when she’s alone.

### rwm · `g4ela-14-3-rwm.mp3` · 64 words

This sentence is about me, and I’ll own it. I heard her, and I crept in. Notice the writer didn’t say I walked, or marched, the way I generally do. Crept is a careful word. It tells you somebody was trying very hard not to make things worse. When you write yours, choose that verb slowly. One right word can say a great deal.

### guide3 · `g4ela-14-3-guide3.mp3` · 68 words

Now you write. Take your time with these. One asks what happened that morning, one asks why Anne wanted to be alone, and one asks about laughing again. That last one surprised her. She told Mrs. Allan, not me, and I don’t blame her. Answer each one plain and true, with something from the book to show it. Then rest a minute before the workshop. I’d recommend it.
