This Week at a Glance

The last three chapters. Peter challenges Miraz to single combat so the two armies won’t have to fight. In the duel, Miraz trips and Peter steps back instead of striking; then Miraz’s own lords betray him and the battle begins, until the trees arrive. Aslan and the girls travel the country waking it up. Caspian is made King, the Telmarines are offered a door home, and the four children step back onto the train platform where the story began. Each day your student also writes a short Writing Connection, and Day 4 is the final literary response for the book.

💛 A heads-up for families
Chapter 14 has the duel and a short battle: Miraz is killed by one of his own lords, who blames the Narnians. Chapter 15 brings a goodbye: Peter and Susan learn they will not come back to Narnia. Some children feel that one.
Reading skill
How the ending reveals theme. Your student names what the story still has to settle, watches how it gets settled, and says what the ending shows the book believes.
Big idea
Honour, and letting go. Peter wins the right way, Caspian is crowned because he knows he isn’t ready, and Peter and Susan say goodbye.

“If you had felt yourself sufficient, it would have been a proof that you were not.”Aslan, Chapter 15

Words to Know

Use them at home!
challengean invitation to fight that must be answered
marshalan official who oversees a contest and keeps order
sufficientenough for what is needed
coronationthe ceremony of crowning a king or queen
Word parts
A review of every base in the unit, plus press (push), tain (hold) and lev (lift).port · tract · struct · spect · ject · script · rupt · duct · form
Spelling
Cumulative review. A hard word is two or three easy rules stacked up. Take it apart and use them one at a time.hope + ful · forget + t + able
Grammar
Dialogue punctuation. Quotation marks go around the exact words; the comma or end mark goes inside them; the tag joins with a comma.“Well!” said Peter. “We have had a time.”

Writing This Week

Writing Connection

The book’s last writing. Day 1 is three or four sentences on why Peter’s challenge letter uses such formal words; Days 2 and 3 practice Lewis’s patterns (Trumpkin’s excited fragment, and Aslan’s balanced “both … enough to …, and … enough to …”); Day 4 is the final literary response, a five-to-seven-sentence paragraph with three pieces of evidence from the whole book.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. Why did Peter fight Miraz himself instead of with the whole army?Listen for: Caspian was wounded, and one fight could spare many lives.
  2. What did Peter do when Miraz tripped?Listen for: he stepped back and waited for Miraz to get up.
  3. Who really killed Miraz?Listen for: his own lords, Glozelle and Sopespian, who then blamed the Narnians.
  4. Why did Aslan say Caspian was ready to be King?Listen for: Caspian said he didn’t feel sufficient, and Aslan said that was the proof.
  5. How did the Mice win Reepicheep his tail back?Listen for: they were ready to cut off their own tails so he wouldn’t be alone.
  6. Which pillar did the characters show most in the whole book?Listen for: a pillar and a real moment from anywhere in Prince Caspian.
