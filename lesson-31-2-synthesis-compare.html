📚 What Good Readers Do
Your reading lens for today

Good readers identify patterns across stories. Yesterday you defined your theme. Today, be specific: find the moments in the novels where your theme appears. Look for what characters say, what they do, and what they sacrifice.

The strongest comparisons use specific evidence, not general feelings. Not “Anne was brave” — but what did Anne DO that showed bravery?

🧠 As you work today, ask yourself:

What is one scene — one actual moment in the story — where your theme shows up? What did the character say or do? Why does that moment prove your theme better than just describing it in general?

📌 What These Words Mean

indignant (Anne) — feeling angry or annoyed because something is unfair. Anne felt indignant when Mrs. Lynde criticized her hair.
vexed (Anne) — troubled or irritated. Marilla was vexed when Anne forgot her chores.
solemnly (NTS) — in a serious, formal, or deeply sincere way. Annemarie solemnly promised to be brave.
occupation (NTS) — the control of a country by a military force of another nation. During the German occupation, the Jews of Denmark were in danger.
allegiance (PC) — loyalty or commitment to a leader, country, or cause. The Old Narnians swore allegiance to Prince Caspian.
sovereign (PC) — a supreme ruler; having complete power. Aslan was the sovereign over all of Narnia.

✏️ Grammar: Spiral Review — Identify the Rule
Spot the grammar concept at work in each sentence from your novels.
📌 Grammar Rules We’ve Studied

Proper noun capitalization — names of people, places, and specific titles are always capitalized.
Comma in dialogue — a comma separates the speaker tag from the spoken words.
Quotation marks — go around the exact words someone speaks.
Comma in a series — use commas between items in a list of three or more.
Apostrophe for possession — add ’s to show something belongs to someone.

🔠 Spelling: Year-End Pattern Sort
Sort 10 words into three spelling pattern categories from the year.
📌 Three Suffix Rules

Doubling Rule — for short-vowel words ending in one consonant: double it before adding -ing or -ed. plan → planning, stop → stopped
Drop-E Rule — for words ending in silent e: drop the e before adding -ing or -ly. write → writing, brave → bravely
Change-Y Rule — for words ending in consonant + y: change the y to i before adding -est, -ed, or -es. happy → happiest, cry → cried

✍️ RWM — Read Like a Reader, Read Like a Writer, Mimic
“Anne came dancing home in the purple winter twilight across the snowy places.”
📖 Read Like a Reader
What picture does this sentence paint? Where is Anne and how is she feeling? What time of day is it? Why is she dancing?
✏️ Read Like a Writer
Notice the string of descriptive details L.M. Montgomery stacks: “purple winter twilight” and “snowy places.” How does stacking three or four descriptions in a row create a vivid image? What would be lost if the sentence just said “Anne walked home at dusk”?
