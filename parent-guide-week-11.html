  • 🗂️Four tabs, in order: Warm-Up → Word Study → Reading → Assignment. A button at the bottom of each tab moves to the next.
  • 💾Answers save as you type, but only in that browser on that computer. Switching computers or clearing the browser loses them.
  • 📋Everything is gathered at the end. The bottom of the Assignment tab collects every answer from the lesson in one place.
  • 📄Download as PDF saves that work as a “My Work” journal page. Use it (or Copy) to submit in Canvas. A lesson isn’t turned in until it’s submitted there.

This Week at a Glance

Anne bakes a cake for the new minister’s wife, Mrs. Allan, and flavors it with liniment by mistake. She takes Josie Pye’s dare to walk along a rooftop and falls, breaking her ankle. Then a new teacher, Miss Stacy, arrives and changes school for Anne, and shy Matthew buys Anne the dress with puffed sleeves she has always wanted. This is also the week your student finishes the first draft of their story.

Reading skill
How setting shapes events. Your student looks at how a place (a careful tea table, a high roof, a new classroom) changes what happens.
Growing-up theme
Being seen. Mrs. Allan, Miss Stacy, and Matthew each notice who Anne really is. Also: knowing when “brave” is really just being dared.

“Marilla, isn’t it nice to think that tomorrow is a new day with no mistakes in it yet?”Anne Shirley, Chapter 21

Words to Know

Use them at home!
vivaciouslively; full of energy and good spirits
kindredalike in spirit; sharing the same feelings, ideas, or imagination
precariousunsafe; dangerously unsteady
exquisitebeautiful in a delicate, refined way; very fine
Word parts
anti- means “against.” de- means “down” or “away.”antifreeze · antibody · depart · descend
Spelling
y to i. Consonant + y changes to i before a suffix. Keep the y after a vowel, or before -ing.happier · played · carrying
Grammar
Past and future verbs. Past already happened (often -ed). Future uses will + the verb.baked · bakes · will bake

By Heart: This Unit’s Poem

Who has seen the wind?
Neither I nor you:
But when the leaves hang trembling,
The wind is passing through.Christina Rossetti

This is the poem your student is memorizing across the Anne unit. It comes up on Days 2 and 4, and each time a few more words fade from the screen.

How to help: Ask your student to say it to you without looking. If they joined this week, that’s fine. The poem starts from the full text for every new student.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. How did liniment end up in Mrs. Allan’s cake?Listen for: Marilla poured leftover liniment into an old vanilla bottle, and Anne had a cold and couldn’t smell it.
  2. Why did Anne walk the roof when she knew it was dangerous?Listen for: Josie dared her, the other girls were watching, and Anne said her “honor” was at stake.
  3. What makes Miss Stacy different from Anne’s old teacher?Listen for: field trips, recitations, the concert, and how she makes Anne feel noticed.
  4. Why do you think it was Matthew who noticed about the puffed sleeves?Listen for: Matthew watches quietly and pays attention, even though he hardly talks.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Yesterday, today, tomorrow. Say one thing you did, one thing you do, and one thing you will do, all with the same verb.
  • y to i. Turn baby, try, play, funny into babies, tried, played, funnier. Which keep their y, and why?
  • Who sees you? Talk about a grown-up who really noticed you, the way Miss Stacy notices Anne.
