This Week at a Glance

Caspian’s army of Old Narnians holds its council, retreats to an ancient mound called Aslan’s How, and starts losing the war. On the worst night, Caspian blows Queen Susan’s Horn, and Trumpkin goes to meet whoever answers. That is how the Dwarf’s story ends. Then the four children set out to find Caspian, and at the edge of a gorge Lucy sees Aslan when no one else does. The group votes, and she loses. Each day your student also writes a short Writing Connection.

💛 A heads-up for families
Chapter 7 describes Caspian’s side losing battles: a Bear and a Centaur are badly hurt. Nikabrik offers to kill Doctor Cornelius, and Caspian stands up for his old tutor. No one is killed in these chapters.
Reading skill
Conflict and leadership choices. Your student names who is set against whom, what is at stake, and the moment someone has to decide.
Big idea
What a leader does when the choice is hard. Caspian at the council, Peter at the gorge, and Edmund standing up for his sister.

“I know the difference between giving advice and taking orders.”Trumpkin, Chapter 7

Words to Know

Use them at home!
councila group gathered to talk and decide
entrenchmentsditches and banks dug for defense
consortscompanions, especially of a king or queen
gorgea deep, narrow valley with steep sides
Word parts
Latin bases: spect (look), ject (throw), script / scribe (write).respect · eject · scribe
Spelling
The schwa sound. The lazy “uh” in an unstressed syllable can’t be heard, so find a related word where that vowel is loud.opposite (from oppose)
Grammar
Complex sentences. A clause that starts with because, although, when or if can’t stand alone; it leans on the main clause. When it comes first, a comma follows it.If we start at once, we can be at the head of Glasswater before dark.

Writing This Week

Writing Connection

This week builds toward a claim paragraph. Day 1 is two or three sentences about a time a group couldn’t agree; Days 2 and 3 practice Lewis’s sentence patterns (a string of “Do you remember…” questions, and one plain sentence that shows a choice); Day 4 is a full paragraph (five to seven sentences) about what makes a good leader, with two pieces of evidence from Chapters 7–9.

Ask at Home

Better than “How was school?”
  1. What did the different groups want at the council?Listen for: the Bears wanted supper first, the Mice wanted to attack at once, the Moles wanted to dig, the Fauns wanted to dance.
  2. Why did Caspian wait so long to blow the Horn?Listen for: he was told to use it only at his greatest need, and he worried there might be a worse need later.
  3. Why did Trumpkin go to Cair Paravel if he didn’t believe in the Horn?Listen for: Caspian was his King; he gave his advice, then followed orders.
  4. How did the children prove to Trumpkin who they were?Listen for: Edmund’s sword fight, Susan’s archery, Lucy healing his shoulder.
  5. How did each person vote at the gorge, and was Peter right?Listen for: Edmund voted with Lucy; Peter, Susan and Trumpkin voted to go down. Any opinion, with a reason.
  6. Which pillar did you see most this week?Listen for: a pillar and a real moment from Chapters 7–9.

Try at Home

5 minutes
  • Hold a family council about something small (dinner, a weekend plan). Afterward, talk about who decided and how.
  • Try a fair vote where everyone gives a reason first, like the children at the gorge.
  • Talk about a time someone in the family was right when no one believed them
