✏ Grammar: Prepositional Phrases
Explain why they matter in writing.

A prepositional phrase starts with a preposition (like in, on, through, after, with, across, during) and ends with a noun. It tells WHERE, WHEN, or HOW something happens.

📌 Why Writers Use Them

Without prepositional phrases, writing is flat: “Harriet moved.”

With prepositional phrases, writing comes alive: “Harriet moved through the night, across the frozen river, with a lantern in her hand.”

Sterling stacks prepositional phrases to put readers inside Harriet’s world — you feel where she is, when it happens, and how she moves.

Writer’s Workshop: Week 24 · Q4 Phase 5 — Conclusion
Write a 3-move conclusion for your research essay
✓ Research Question ✓ Gather Notes ✓ Organize + Draft ✓ Elaborate w/ Facts ★ Write Conclusion ○ Revise + Edit
🎯

Today’s focus: Write the conclusion for your research essay. A strong conclusion does three things: it restates the main idea (in new words), summarizes your strongest evidence, and ends with a lasting thought — why this matters.

👁 The 3-Move Conclusion

Move 1: Restate — Say your main idea again in different words. Do NOT copy your introduction.

Move 2: Summarize — Point back to your strongest pieces of evidence. One or two sentences: “From ___ to ___, the evidence shows that…”

Move 3: Lasting Thought — End with something worth thinking about. Why does this still matter? What does the reader carry away?

📌 Sentence Frames to Help You Start

Move 1: “Throughout her life, Harriet Tubman showed that…”  |  “The evidence in this essay reveals that…”

Move 2: “From ___ to ___, we see that…”  |  “Whether she was ___ or ___, Harriet always…”

 Move 3: “Today, Harriet’s story reminds us that…”  |  “The freedom she carried was not just her own —”

Write your 3-move conclusion. Aim for 4–6 sentences. Label the three moves if it helps you check your work.
